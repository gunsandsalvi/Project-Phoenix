# Item 11.2 — One bank: one balance sheet, one treasury, one face to every market

## The review

A bank in this codebase is not one decider. It is FIVE, in five modules, each given a slice of the
bank by the spec system that module implements — and each acting under the bank's name with no
knowledge of the others:

| who decides for the bank | where | what it posts or pays |
|---|---|---|
| a **credit** decider | `bank-lending` | loan quotes, the overdraft door, the capital position, the raise |
| a **liquidity-buffer** decider | `sovereign-curve` | **buy and sell orders** in every sovereign line, from a buffer target |
| a **primary-dealer** decider | `sovereign-auction` | bids in the auction, from an obligation and the same buffer |
| a **forced-seller** decider | `money-market/funding.ts` | **sell orders** in any eligible line when the session refused it |
| a **funding** decider | `money-market` | its session schedule, its deposit rates, its window draw |
| and a phantom sixth: the **desk**, a separate PARTY | `dealers` | two-way quotes, its own account, its own equity, its own death |

Three of those post ORDERS into secondary markets under one bank's name. The consequences are not
edge cases; they are the bugs this project has been patching one at a time:

- **A bank trades against itself.** The buffer decider can post a buy in a sovereign line in the same
  period the forced-seller posts a sell in it. Item 11 met this and patched the SOLVER (`pairFills`
  "never pairs a party with itself") — a symptom patch in the kernel for a defect in the bank (Law 12).
- **Two reasons for one demand.** `sovereign-curve`'s participant stands down whenever an auction is
  on because the auction participant "bids through its obligation" — the code says out loud that two
  deciders want the same paper for two reasons and picks one by hand.
- **The treasury makes markets.** The buffer decider posts two-way in every sovereign line every
  period at required yield ± a premium. A treasury does not make markets. It holds a liquidity
  portfolio and buys or sells into one.
- **Two buffers.** `bufferOf` (money-market: cash against the worst week) and `bufferTarget`
  (sovereign-curve: paper against deposits) are one treasury's one liquidity decision, held in two
  modules with two derivations — and the second is the engine's last placeholder.
- **The ladder is in pieces.** D1's "it shrinks: sells assets and stops originating" is one
  treasurer's one decision; here selling is a money-market participant, stopping is a lending read,
  bidding up is a deposit routine, and none of them knows the others ran.
- **A desk that is a party.** Written down as a creditor of its own bank in a resolution; consuming
  none of its bank's capital, so the weighted requirement asked the bank for nothing; paying a rent
  to itself; walking to a rival for two basis points; treated by an XI-2 attempt as a counterparty
  with a line. Dealer Desks F2 ("no desk exempt from its own bank's capital and funding") was being
  broken by construction.
- **The obligation belongs to the wrong owner.** `sovereign.primaryDealers.minBidShare` is declared
  in the auction module, owner "model": it is the issuer's term of the dealership, and the bank
  should read it off the offer.

The root is architectural: **modules were cut along spec systems, and spec systems are requirements,
not deciders.** §23, §24, §25, §26 and §8.E all describe ONE bank from different sides. Mapping each
to a module, and each module to a participant, turned one bank into a committee that never meets.

## The principles this item builds to

1. **One bank is one party, one balance sheet, one account at the central bank, one equity.** Its
   lines of business — treasury, lending, dealing; syndicate/IBD (13f) and prime brokerage (13h)
   later — are BOOKS inside it, told apart by intent, with no account, equity or death of their own.
2. **Decisions live with the decider.** A venue module clears what is posted; an instrument module
   says what a kind is; the bank module decides everything a bank decides. `money-market` is the
   market BETWEEN banks and the central bank (venues, corridor, rows, the insurer, the resolution),
   and it no longer decides anything for a bank.
3. **One face to every market.** The bank's DEALING line is the only thing that ever posts into a
   market — secondary or primary — and it posts one schedule per book per period. Treasury never
   posts. Treasury's intents reach the market as the desk's TARGET inventory and its urgency, which
   is how a real treasury sells its liquidity portfolio: through its own desk, whose quote then skews.
4. **Treasury owns the balance sheet.** Funding (deposit rates, the session schedule, the window as
   last resort), liquidity (how much to hold and in what form), capital (the position and its
   allocation across lines), and the raise. It is the only line that talks to the money market.

## Design

### 1. The `banks` module

`bank-lending`, `dealers`, the bank participants of `sovereign-curve` and `sovereign-auction`,
`money-market/funding.ts`, and the bank's own decisions in `money-market/deposits.ts` and
`session.ts` fold into ONE module, `mechanisms/banks/`:

```
banks/
  data.ts        the bank rows (existing BANKS + FUNDERS merged: one row per bank), the lines each has
  treasury.ts    liquidity target, funding schedule, deposit rates, the ladder, allocation, the raise
  lending.ts     the credit decision (quote.ts today), the overdraft door, loan rows
  dealing.ts     the quote (dealers/quote.ts, near-unchanged), targets, the primary bid, the AP role
  capital.ts     the position: banking book and trading book, both rules, which binds
  index.ts       phases, the ONE participant (partyKind: BANK), publications
```

Everything the world reads about a bank is published by this module under the bank's name:
`bank.costOfFunds`, `bank.reservation`, `bank.capital`, `bank.liquidity`, `bank.depositRate`,
`bank.dealing` (was `dealers.book`), `bank.lines`.

### 2. Treasury

- **Liquidity.** ONE decision replaces two: the treasury holds liquid assets against what could leave
  (Banks Funding C2) — its own worst week (`bufferOf`, kept) in CASH, and paper above that as the
  liquidity portfolio, sized by the regulator's floor where one exists and the bank's own preference
  above it. The form is a decision: reserves earn the floor, paper earns its yield and is pledgeable.
  It sets a TARGET holding per liquidity kind (sovereign bills and bonds) and hands the target to the
  dealing line. It posts nothing. `sovereign-curve` keeps the curve, `priceAtYield` and
  `demandSteps` (arithmetic, moved to `prices/curve.ts` where the kernel's curve lives); its bank
  participant and `bufferTarget` are deleted, and with them `P_BUFFER` — the placeholder's death is
  the treasury owning the decision, not a new formula (whether the target's SIZE is a policy floor
  plus a preference is 11.2's measurement).
- **Funding.** The deposit-rate decision (`setRates`, `defended`, `worthOfMoney`) and the session
  schedule (`bankOrders`: what it lends to which name at what rate, what it borrows) move here.
  The money market keeps the venues, the corridor, `strike`, `writeRow`, the window's own offer and
  the depositors' moving (`moveDeposits` is the DEPOSITORS' decision, dispatched by class table, and
  stays a market-side routine until each depositor kind's module owns its own — noted, not done).
- **The ladder as one decision, in one place, in D1's order** (Money Market D, Banks Funding D):
  borrow in the session → sell or pledge liquid assets → bid up deposits → stop originating → draw
  the window → fail. Each rung is a treasury instruction to a line: "sell X of paper" lowers the
  dealing line's sovereign target and marks it urgent (its offer goes to the bid side); "stop
  originating" is the lending line's allocation going to zero; "bid up" is its own rate decision.
  The refusal (B7) remains the session's public event; what the treasury does about it is its own.
- **Capital allocation across lines.** Each line's realised return on the capital it used is a
  private read (`bank.lines`: lending — interest less losses booked, over capital at the credit
  weight; dealing — spread earned less what the inventory did (F3), over capital at the trading
  weight). The treasury gives the published headroom to the line with the higher realised return
  over the bank's own memory, up to what it asks; the other takes the rest; no floor. Period zero:
  phase order (lending, then dealing), stated in the record. Costs and headcount per line are not
  here — banks employ nobody yet (13d, 13f).
- **The raise** (`subordinated.ts`) is treasury's, unchanged.

### 3. Dealing

- **One quote, around a target.** `dealers/quote.ts` moves across: it never read anything but the
  desk's own state, and that state is now the bank's. The one change: the skew is measured from the
  TARGET inventory, not from flat. For a liquidity kind the target is treasury's; for everything
  else it is zero. Above target it bids and offers lower (it wants to sell); below, higher. That is
  C2.a's "how a desk mean-reverts its book without anyone telling it to" — with the treasury telling
  it where the book should be, once, as a number it owns.
- **Urgency.** A treasury sale under the ladder is the offer at the bid side (`price: 'market'` on
  the sell, size = what treasury asked for): a forced sale fetches what somebody posted (Clearing C3,
  XI-2). The buy side is withdrawn that period in that book — a bank liquidating does not bid.
- **The primary auction.** The dealing line bids as primary dealer: its obligation (read off the
  OFFER — the issuer publishes the dealership share with the announcement; the param moves to the
  treasury's own module, owner `treasury`) plus what treasury's target is short of, at the yield the
  bank requires. One bid, one reason to hold it (sovereign-curve's stand-down rule deleted).
- **The AP role.** The ETF is launched by and created/redeemed by the banks' dealing lines
  (`funds/data.ts launchedBy`; `arbitrage` moves here).
- **Funded at the blended cost of funds** (`bank.costOfFunds`, one writer; the spec says blended,
  B2.a) plus the capital charge at the trading weight — the rent, its phase and its family go.
- **Limits.** The aggregate limit is the dealing line's ALLOCATION (above); the concentration limit
  per line is a ratio of it (`bank.dealing.concentration.<bank>`, preference). The desk's money
  limits and its own `requiredReturnOnCapital` (a second copy of the bank's) are deleted.

### 4. Capital: banking book and trading book, by intent, on one balance sheet

For a kind the dealing line makes: the part of the bank's holding INSIDE the treasury's target is
banking book and weighs what a claim on that issuer weighs (zero for a party that cannot fail, item
11's rule); the part OUTSIDE the target — above it or short of it — is trading book and weighs
`regulation.riskWeight.tradingBook` (declared by item 9, read for the first time). Every other
holding is banking book. So a bank running a big equity inventory has weighted assets, and B1.a's
"risk weights differ by asset, and that is why a bank prefers some assets to others" has something
to bite on. The two rules and which binds are unchanged.

### 5. The kernel: one line, and it is a deletion

The door this item needs — a venue asking parties for schedules the way a market does — is **11.1**,
inserted ahead of this item and closed before it opens, because the kernel changes only by an
inserted item. The treasury posts its session schedule through that door (`venueParticipants`), and
the money market calls `gather` at the top of `runSession`.

What this item itself touches in the kernel is ONE line, and it removes code: **`pairFills` stops
skipping a self-cross.** Nobody trades with itself is a CONTRACT (Clearing A2, Register D2): a party
on both sides of one book at crossing prices is a defect in that party's module and throws at the
site. Item 11 patched the solver to advance past it because a bank really could post both sides;
with one face per bank the case is unreachable, and the patch would only hide the next module that
reintroduces it. It cannot be its own item: on its own it is red, because the cause it is the fix
for is this item (Law 12 — a cause has one fix and it removes code). Named here so the record says
why a module item deleted a kernel line.

### 6. Everything else that moves

- The `DESK` kind, both desk parties, their cash, the rent, `inventoryPaysRent`, `desk` in the
  wholesale deposit class: deleted. The banks open holding the equity float (Seed A3). The
  `moveBook` sweep in the resolution stays for parties that do bank there; `bank-resolution.test.ts`
  stops listing a desk among creditors.
- `BANKS` (lending data) and `FUNDERS` (money-market data) become ONE row per bank in
  `banks/data.ts`: one set of preferences per bank in one place (Law 4).
- Reduced test worlds that filter modules by id (`'bank-lending'`, `'money-market'`,
  `'dealers'`) are updated to `'banks'`; ten-odd files, mechanical.
- ARCHITECTURE §4.9b and §4.11b: modules are cut along DECIDERS and VENUES, and the table says
  which module decides for which party kind.

### Parameters

Deleted: `desk.limit.perInstrument.*`, `desk.limit.aggregate.*`, `desk.requiredReturnOnCapital.*`,
the desks' cash, `bank.liquidityBuffer.perDeposit` (placeholder), `sovereign.holders.surplusPremium`
(a two-step demand schedule was the treasury making a market; the dealing line's skew is the second
step now). Moved: `sovereign.primaryDealers.minBidShare` → the treasury module, owner `treasury`,
published on the offer. New: `bank.dealing.concentration.<bank>` (preference). Merged: the bank rows.

### Audit contributions (all `banks`)

- `accounts`: **the trading book is capitalised** — published RWA ≥ the part of each dealing-line
  holding outside its target, at marks, at the trading weight (Dealer Desks F2).
- `flows`: **one face per market** — in any period, a bank has at most one order set per book, from
  one module, and never both sides at crossing prices (Clearing A2; the kernel throw is the contract,
  this is the measurement).
- `names` (kernel, existing): a `desk.*` party anywhere is an assembly failure.

## Exit criteria

Exactly one module gives `BANK` a participant, in markets and in venues; no function outside
`mechanisms/banks/` reads a bank's preference or decides anything for it; no party of kind desk
exists and every market the desks made is still made two ways by a bank; a bank never posts both
sides of one book at crossing prices and the solver would throw if it did; a bank's sovereign
holdings reach its treasury's target through its own quote; RWA is not zero and which rule binds can
go either way; the ladder's rungs fire in D1's order from one place; the engine has no placeholder;
the year-long squeeze and the resolution suites are green with their claims unchanged.

## Guard

Dealer Desks B4 (the quote reads none of the book — a target is the bank's own state, not the
book's), C5.a/C5.b (no width anywhere), F2, F3; Clearing A2 (nobody trades with itself — now a
throw); Banks Funding B2.b (one rate per class); Law 4 (one row per bank, one buffer, one cost of
funds); Law 6 (no floor on any line's allocation); Law 12 (the solver patch is deleted, not kept
"just in case"); Law 15 (a line is data; nothing branches on a line's name).
