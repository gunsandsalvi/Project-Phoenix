# Item 13d.1 — A cell's key carries its age, its tenure and its wealth

**Objective.** A household cell stands for one possible household with a multiplicity, and what
makes two households different is in its KEY (XI-15). Today the key is `region | cohort | bank`, and
three things 13d needs are not in it: how old the people are, whether they own the roof over them,
and how much they have. Everything 13d could not build ran aground on the same rock, and it is one
rock.

**Why it is one item and not three.** The blocker is identical in all three cases: a cell carries
its holdings PER MEMBER in whole pieces (Law 8), so anything that moves people between two cells has
to move value between two cells — and a leg's `qty` must equal `perMember × weight` on each side at
once, which two weights in the millions share no useful number for. `merge` cannot help: it refuses
two cells whose per-member state differs, which is exactly what handing an estate over makes true.
A weight event alone destroys units, because per-member state stays put while the total falls.

**The answer is that nothing should move.** If the thing that differs is IN THE KEY, then crossing a
boundary is a SPLIT — exact, per-member state and all — and the split cell simply carries a
different key. Nobody appears, nobody disappears, no value crosses, and the register never has to
find a quantity two weights agree on. That is what this item builds, and it is why it is the one
item that unblocks four of 13d's steps.

**Read first.** XI-15 (all, and `world/cells.ts` with it); Households A2, A2.b, F1, F1.a, F1.b, F2,
F3; Housing C1–C4, D1; Labour B3, E4; `registry/registry.ts`'s `CellKeyDimension`; 13b.1's key data.

**Clauses this item meets.** Households A2.b (wealth and tenure as ways households differ), F1, F1.a,
F1.b, F2, F2.a, F3; Housing C1 (a household mortgage), C4 (its foreclosure), D1; Labour E4; XI-15.

### Steps

- [ ] **The key gains three dimensions as DATA** (`CellKeyDimension`): `age` (a band, not a year —
      the band a person is in is a fact and the year inside it is not something this world tracks),
      `tenure` (owns the roof, rents it), `wealth` (a band). Nothing branches on any of them; they
      are dimensions, and what reads them is `keyOf` like everything else.
- [x] **A split by key**: `ctx.cells.reKey(cell, members, key, cause)` — the one new kernel event,
      and it is a SPLIT with a different key on the part that moved, which is the only way a weight
      moves between keys without value moving with it. It refuses a key the registry does not
      declare and a count that is not whole people.
- [x] **Ageing is a re-key** at the band boundary, at the rate the band's own span implies (a SHAPE
      with its death written: an age in the key is a band, so the ages inside it are spread evenly
      until something says otherwise).
- [x] **Retirement is the same event**, at the band the registry says is past working age. Labour's
      `participates` reads the band rather than the cohort.
- [x] **Mortality goes through PROBATE, because a cell cannot pay a cell.** Both sides of a leg on a
      cell must be whole pieces for every member of it, and two cells of different weights have no
      such number — so an estate cannot be handed from the dead to their heirs directly. A NAMED
      party can take a thing to the piece and hand it on, and that is what probate IS: one per place
      and bank, holding what somebody held until it can be divided. The dead split off with their
      own share of everything (a split is exact), hand it over in full, and the cell ends holding
      nothing, which is the only state a cell may end in. `ctx.cells.die` is the kernel event, and
      it REFUSES a cell that still holds something — "no death without a destination" and "no
      residual with no holder" are one rule, enforced where a cell ends rather than in whichever
      module called.
- [x] **A household can hold a roof, and it needed no tenure band at all.** The blocker was never
      tenure: it was that the register's grid had one piece per whole thing, so a person owning four
      tenths of a dwelling or buying a car every ten years rounded to NOTHING per member. The grid
      is a RESOLUTION (Law 2) and it has to be fine enough to say what one member of a cell holds,
      so every good is counted on the same fine grid now and what is indivisible stays indivisible
      where indivisibility lives: occupancy is whole dwellings, a plant vintage is whole units of
      plant. Half the basket 13c.2 built was inert because of this — clothing, furniture,
      appliances, electronics and vehicles all rounded to nothing per member before they reached a
      book — so this fixed more than housing.
- [x] **It was not dust: it was two kernel defects nobody had met, because no cell had ever issued
      anything.** Traced rather than guessed. (1) `payToHolders` denominated the HOLDER's side of a
      coupon per member and left the payer's at `none`, so the first payment a cell owed was refused
      by the settlement validator — whichever side is a cell governs now, and both sides being cells
      throws rather than rounding, because rounding it is where units go missing. (2) Settlement
      booked an issuer's liability at the ISSUED TOTAL against an equity account that is kept PER
      MEMBER — the same number for a named party, and a million households' worth against one
      household's equity for a cell. Both are fixed and both are the kernel's.
- [ ] **A borrower that misses goes on accruing, and its balance sheet drifts.** What still stops a
      household mortgage: the lender carries the loan at amortised cost, so unpaid interest keeps
      raising the liability on ITS book while nothing moves on the borrower's own. A household with
      no money set aside to service a mortgage misses immediately, so it shows there first — but it
      is the same defect for any borrower. Positioned to **13f**, with servicing a mortgage out of a
      household's own budget (Households E3, E4), which is the same item. `housing.asking` walks
      named parties only until then, and says so where it does it.
- [ ] **Labour E4**: a cell that lost its earner misses its mortgage service, through item 5's event.
- [ ] **Wealth as a band** closes Households A2.b: the same income in different hands is different
      demand because the hands differ, and how much they have is one of the ways.
- [ ] Tests: the population is conserved across every re-key; no value crosses a key boundary; a
      band's occupancy moves and its per-member state does not; a cell that owns its roof pays no
      rent and a cell that rents pays somebody named.
- [ ] Coverage re-marked; record entry; delete this file; worklist row → done; **13d resumes**.

### Two findings this item picked up

**A fund gains equity when a holder of its shares is not a cell.** Measured: `households.test.ts`
goes from three reds to five once probate holds ETF shares, and both new ones are
`etf.us has equity of -83.49: a fund with equity has mislaid somebody's money`. A fund's equity is
zero because its liability is its shares and the holders own the assets — "nothing here enforces
that, it falls out of the wire" — so something in the wire treats a named holder differently from a
cell. It is not probate's defect; probate is the first named party ever to hold fund shares.
Positioned to **13h**, which is where funds are worked on next.

**Probate divides slowly in a small world, and that is honest rather than wrong.** One person's
estate spread over a cohort of thousands is less than a piece each, so probate holds it until
enough has accumulated to divide — which is what an estate that cannot be split evenly does. What
would be wrong is rounding it away, and that is exactly what the named party exists to prevent.

### A finding this item picked up

**The rig cannot cover sixty-two lines.** `RIG_FIRMS` is a TOTAL of twelve, spread over the lines by
their real shares, so most lines get none and a test that asks the draw for a machine works is told
"it drew 0" — two reds in `goods.test.ts`, and they pre-date this item. `drawFirms` now takes a
floor per line and the rig has the constant (`RIG_PER_LINE`), set to ZERO with the reason written
where it is declared: raising it changes how many firms a rig world has, which changes the
population the scale model is sized to (`rigMembers`), which changes what a labour venue has in it —
and seven of `labour.test.ts`'s assertions are about a venue with a known number of seekers. Making
every line present means deriving the population from the firms the draw ACTUALLY made rather than
from the number it was asked for. That is one bounded change and it belongs with **16**, where the
scale model is measured.

### Guard

The temptation is to let value cross a key boundary "just this once" — an estate to another cohort's
cell, a dwelling to a household that has just bought one. Every one of those is the defect this item
exists to remove, and the register will refuse it: a leg on a cell must be denominated per member,
and two cells of different weights have no such denomination. If a step here seems to need one, the
key is wrong, not the rule.
