# Item 13b.1 — The checks that do not check

**Objective.** The guards, the lint rules and the audit families were written so that the laws would
not depend on anybody remembering them. Several of them do not fire. `phoenix/no-cross-module-import`
has never reported anything and six modules import each other, including a cycle; `phoenix/no-bounds`
forbids a spelling and the engine writes the same operation thirty-one times in another one; a family
with one built contribution and three stubs reports green; the flows family exempts a party's whole
position because one weight event named it; the register still carries the floating-point tolerance
Law 8 retired, including the one branch in the engine that can destroy units. This item makes the
machinery true again. **Most of it removes code** (Law 12), and nothing in it changes a mechanism,
an economic outcome or a boundary (Law 18): what it changes is what the tools catch.

**Why here.** Inserted after 13b and before 13c. It cannot go earlier: 13b is open and Law 10 says
the open item finishes first, and two of its findings (the contract store's missing validation, the
layer's double-counted capacity) are 13b's own and are folded there. It must not go later: every
item from 13c on adds modules, and the boundary rule that is supposed to keep them apart is the
thing that does not work. The measurement that argues the position is in the record — 13a cost
twenty-four kernel files to seven module files because a new SHAPE of state has to be taught to the
kernel, and four of those causes are avoidable and are fixed here, so 13c–13i pay less for the same
work.

**Read first.** `CLAUDE.md` (the laws, in full — this item is about whether they are enforced);
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4.9b (the module contract), §5 (error discipline), §9 (the lint rules);
`packages/engine/src/core/tick.ts` (the Law 8 argument the register has not caught up with);
`tools/eslint-rules/index.js`. The findings below each name the file and the line.

**Clauses this item meets.** No new clause. It is Law 2, Law 4, Law 5, Law 6, Law 7, Law 8, Law 12,
Law 15, Law 16 and Audit C2/C3 enforced where they were already meant to be.

## Findings this item carries

Every one was measured by reading the file against what the file says it does. Severity as the
review stated it.

### The module boundary is not enforced, and six modules cross it (DEFECT)

`tools/eslint-rules/index.js`, `noCrossModuleImport`: `source.replace(/^(\.\.\/)+/, '')` turns
`'../goods/index.js'` into `'goods/index.js'`, which does not match `/^(mechanisms|seeds)\//`, so
nothing is reported. The only spelling it catches is `'../../mechanisms/goods/index.js'`, which
nobody writes. Verified: `npx eslint packages/engine/src/mechanisms/firms/decide.ts` passes clean
while that file imports nine symbols from `capital-programme` and four from `goods`.

| module | imports | what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `firms` | `goods`, `capital-programme` | values: `goodId`, `goodMarketId`, `goodTerms`, `capacityFrom`, `capitalChargePerUnit`, `capitalKindOf`, `isPlant`, `lifeParam`, `plantTerms`, `serviceLeft`, `vintagesHeld`, `costOfDraw`, `dueFromLine`, `wipId`, `plantHeld`, `utilisation` |
| `capital-programme` | `goods` | values: `costOfDraw`, `goodId`, `isGoodTerms` |
| `households` | `goods` | values: `goodId`, `goodMarketId` |
| `treasury` | `goods`, `sovereign-curve`, `sovereign-instruments` | values: `isGoodTerms`, `CURVE_DAY_COUNT`, terms constructors |
| `banks` | `spot-fx` | **a parameter**: `FX_SPREAD` from `spot-fx/data.ts` |
| `spot-fx` | `banks` | types: `BankDecl`, in four files |

`banks ↔ spot-fx` is a cycle, which is the shape that left `PROTECTED` undefined at module-init
during 13b (`cds/contract.ts → participants.ts → series.ts → contract.ts`, surfacing as
`unit undefined does not exist`). `FX_SPREAD` is the worst on its own terms: one behaviour-shaping
number, declared by one module, read by another — Law 4 broken at the parameter register.

Four of the six are the same crossing: `goodId` and `goodMarketId` are naming functions, which is
what `registry/naming.ts` is for. Once the goods naming grammar is kernel data, four modules stop
knowing about a fifth.

### `no-bounds` forbids a spelling (DEFECT)

It reports `Math.min`/`Math.max` and identifiers matching `/^clamp|^saturate|^bound(ed)?$/i`. The
engine writes the same operation as a ternary thirty-one times:

```
labour/matching.ts:221        const taken = people < available ? people : available;
funds/index.ts:340            const budget = wanted > cash ? cash : wanted;
banks/dealing.ts:306          return want < free ? want : free;
derivative-layer/index.ts:175 return affordable < wanted ? affordable : wanted;
households/consume.ts:121     const affordable = wanted > budget ? budget : wanted;
```

**Most are correct** — Law 6's one admissible case, arithmetic impossibility. Two checked closely
(`banks/treasury.ts`'s `c.floor` and `defended`'s `stopAt`) are real alternatives a party has,
argued at length. That is the problem: the rule cannot tell them apart, so a reviewer grepping for
bounds finds nothing and a reviewer grepping for ternaries finds thirty-one with no way to sort them.

### Law 8 retired the dust and the register still carries it (DEFECT)

`register/register.ts` `debit`:

```ts
if (remaining === 0 && h.lots.length > 0) {
  const left = sum(h.lots.map((l) => l.qty));
  if (left.value <= dust) h.lots.length = 0;     // destroys a positive holding
}
```

That deletes units with no instruction and no counterparty (Appendix B: no residual with no holder;
Law 5: every flow has two sides). It was true when quantities were continuous. Every lot quantity
now passes `onTheGrid` at every door in that same file, so a residue is a whole number of pieces and
the smallest is 1, while `dust` is `dustOf(lots + 2, |qty| + |free|)` ≈ `3 × 2.2e-16 × magnitude` —
below 1 for any magnitude under ~1e15. **The branch is unreachable in this world and destroys units
where it becomes reachable.** The same argument kills `deliverable`'s second clause, the `take`
selection, both `if (remaining <= dust) remaining = 0` lines, `deliveryDust` itself, `moneyDelta`'s
`encumbered - after <= dustOf(2, ...)`, `settlement.ts`'s `precheck` money branch and
`validateCellSide`'s comparison, and the money family's B3.c walk tolerance. `core/tick.ts` states
it outright: integers "add, subtract and compare EXACTLY... the checks that compare it need no
tolerance at all rather than a derived one".

### The stores that do not guard what they say they guard (DEFECT)

- **`Instruments.restate` does not invalidate `all()`.** Every other mutator ends
  `this.everything = undefined`; the share-split door does not. After a split, `all()` returns frozen
  records carrying the PRE-split `issued` for the rest of the run — to the ownership family, the
  observer, and every derived value. The memo IS a second register, which is what that file's own
  comment two fields up says an index must never be.
- **`stateEquity` dates every opening entry to period 0**, including for a firm incorporated in
  period 40 or an estate opened mid-run. It takes no period. `equityEntries(party, from, to)` over
  any later span then omits the opening, and Reporting G2's "Σ every entry is the balance" holds only
  when the whole history is asked for.
- **`toTick`, `downTick`, `upTick` cast to `Qty` without `Number.isSafeInteger`.** `asQty` checks it
  and `onTick` checks it; three of the four doors do not, so `upTick(2 ** 60)` returns a `Qty` that
  is not a count of pieces and throws later at a site that did not make the error.
- **`splitOnTick` has a silent `break`** where an `Impossible` belongs: if it ever runs, the parts sum
  to LESS than the total and nothing says so — the exact defect that file's header promises the
  arithmetic "cannot violate instead of something the audit reports afterwards". Unreachable today;
  it is a `noUncheckedIndexedAccess` appeasement written as a silent failure.
- **`Register.forget`** deletes a party's holdings, equity account and ledger without checking any of
  it is empty, on a comment saying the caller has verified — the one door in that file that trusts
  its caller instead of guarding the store.
- **The registry validates instrument kinds six ways and derivative kinds not at all.** No check that
  `priceTick > 0`, none that `DerivativeKindProfile.unit` is a declared unit, so a module registering
  a kind with an undeclared unit fails inside `subdivision` during a price conversion with
  `unit undefined does not exist` and no indication of which kind asked. `tickShift` is never
  validated either: zero makes every tick `Infinity`.
- **`months(n)`** does not check `n` is a positive whole number, and `Calendar.schedule` loops forever
  on `months(0)` — a hang, which is the worst failure this engine can have because it reports nothing.

### The wire does not use `Qty`, and `core/tick.ts` says it does (DEFECT)

That file's argument names its call sites: "Everything that CARRIES a quantity asks for this type:
an order's size, **a leg's amount**, **a lot**, **what is issued**, what a seed endows." Three of
those five are plain `number`: `MoneyLeg.amount`, `AssetLeg.qty`, `CreateLeg.qty`, `DestroyLeg.qty`,
`PledgeLeg.qty`, `CellSide.perMember`, `Lot.qty`, `Instrument.issued`. So the guarantee advertised as
compiler-checked is enforced at one of the four doors, and what catches a fractional leg is the
runtime `onTheGrid` in the register — the far-end guard the `Qty` doc says was the problem.
`downToGrain`/`toGrain` drop the brand too, and `scaleQty`'s `times` is not checked to be whole.

### The audit reports green over things nobody built (DEFECT)

- `Audit.run` sets `built = contributions.some((c) => c.built)` and lists
  `contributions.filter((c) => c.built)`. **Confirmed instance:** `standardFamilies` registers
  `crossMarketFamily()` from `unbuilt.ts` with `built: false`, and the derivative layer registers a
  built one; the family reads green and the kernel's unbuilt check vanishes from the report entirely.
  Audit C2 says an unbuilt family says so and is never green by omission; at the CONTRIBUTION level,
  which is what modules contribute at, it is green by omission.
- **The flows family exempts a party's entire position for a period because one weight event named
  it.** The untracked change is narrow (`copyMemberState` copies per-member state to a new cell
  without an instruction); the exemption is total, so an entry, a death or a promotion — which copy
  nothing — remove Money D3 from every line that cell holds. Households are cells and their weights
  move constantly. Audit C3 says every family runs the same checks every period. The split
  restatement two blocks up is the right shape: read the ratio off the public event, exempt nothing.
- Money's C4.c check is skipped in the first period, so the seed's issuance — the largest in the run
  — is the one nothing verifies.
- The `Audit` constructor throws bare `Error` twice, in the one file whose subject is that every
  finding names its clause.

### Two readers of one fact (DEFECT)

- **Staleness.** `Valuation.worthOf` reads a cleared line with `prices.latest`; `markPerUnit` — which
  `valueOfLots`, `valueAtMark`, `carryingPerUnit`, `equityDust`, settlement's `reseat` and every
  balance-sheet read go through — uses `printOrThrow`. A fund's NAV and the audit's read of the same
  holding use different rules for the same fact.
- **Overdrafts.** `Settlement.apply` passes `allowNegative: true` at both money sites
  unconditionally, so `moneyDelta`'s guard never runs for any instruction in a running world. Its own
  comment says it is "not a second tolerance beside the one settlement uses" and that is exactly what
  it is: a second computation of one question, in a second place, that the only caller disables.
- `Calendar.place` is `periodOf` under a second name, with a doc describing a rule it does not
  compute.
- `Valuation.valueOfLots` accumulates with `+=` and drops its dust, in the one class whose
  `equityDust` claims to derive the tolerance from the arithmetic that produced the number.

### The declarations that are not true (DEFECT / SHAPE)

- **`loan.operatingCost` is a wage bill that is charged and never paid.** Declared
  `kind: 'technology'`, `0.005 per annum on the principal`, with its own `why` saying it is "the
  people, the assessment, the collecting"; read in one place (`banks/quote.ts:146`, added into the
  rate the bank quotes) and paid to nobody. It is margin wearing the clothes of a cost, a placeholder
  declared as a technology, and a cost stated as a share of money — which is what
  `phoenix/no-value-recipe` refuses on the production side. **The mechanism it stands in for is built
  at 13d** (a bank employs people); the DECLARATION is wrong today and is corrected here.
- **`ParamRegister`'s `namesAnItem` guard only fires when `kind === 'shape'`**, so a placeholder
  mislabelled `technology` — which is exactly what `loan.operatingCost` is — sails through. The guard
  polices the honest mistake and misses the one that matters.
- **`ParamDecl.unit` is a free string that nothing ever reads.** The constructor checks it is
  non-empty; `get(id)` returns a bare `number`. So the one place in the engine where every
  behaviour-shaping number is declared with its unit is also the one place the unit cannot be checked,
  and a parameter declared in 'periods' multiplied by a day count is wrong at no site.
- **`cellKey` is declared as data, validated as data, and read by nothing.** The registry checks it
  has no repeats and includes `region`; `CellKey` in `party.ts` hard-codes all three dimensions and
  `Parties.add` validates all three unconditionally. So "lifting a relationship into the key is a data
  change" is false — it is a kernel type change. **This blocks 13d's wealth dimension**, which is the
  clearest argument for fixing it.
- `quotedAs?: 'money' | 'rate'` with "absent means money" is `?? 0` in the type system;
  `contractValueTo` returns `0` for a party on neither side of a contract; `percent(0)` renders as
  `"%"`; `moneyInstrumentId`/`fxPairId` build composite ids by concatenation with no escaping;
  `banks/index.ts:309` writes through a `readonly` with a cast.

### The four avoidable causes of the kernel churn (SEAM)

Measured on the three commits that built item 13: 13a (a new SHAPE of state) cost **24 kernel files
to 7 module files**; 13b (seven CLASSES of that shape) cost **6 to 24**. The architecture is
plug-and-play for instances and costly for shapes, and most of the difference between 24 and about
12 is these four:

1. **`core/ids.ts` grows a brand per module** (`CohortId`, `CurveFamilyId`, `VenueId`, `ContractId`,
   `DerivativeKindId`). A module cannot brand its own identifier type.
2. **`MarketRunDeps.admits` and `marginLegs`** are threaded through the kernel's market runner for
   one module.
3. **`MarketDecl` is three market kinds in one optional-field bag** (`kind?`, `contract?`, `fx?`), so
   `contractTrade` and `fxTrade` each open with a runtime `throw` guarding a state the type could
   forbid, and `delivers()` has to know both module shapes.
4. **`DerivativeKindProfile.orders` put participation in the registry**, so `registry/` now imports
   `world/context.ts` and `clearing/` — and it contradicts that same file's argument two interfaces
   up, where `ContractReads` is documented as public reads and "no view of anybody".

And `SeedContext` hands modules the raw `Parties`, `Instruments` and `Register` — the write-capable
classes, not the facades that exist beside them — so the module contract's "never holds a reference
to a kernel store" is false for that one context. Today's seed does not abuse it.

## Design

Six groups, each one subject, in the order that unblocks the most.

### 1. The boundary (`tools/eslint-rules`, six modules)

Resolve the import against the importing file's directory and compare real paths, rather than
stripping `../` and testing for a prefix. Then the six crossings: `goodId`, `goodMarketId` and
`goodTermsOf` become kernel naming (`registry/naming.ts` is what a naming grammar is for) so four
modules stop importing `goods`; `FX_SPREAD` is declared by whichever module owns it and read by the
other through `params`; `BankDecl` in `spot-fx` becomes a kernel read; `capital-programme`'s
`costOfDraw` is either kernel arithmetic or the read moves to the context. The `banks ↔ spot-fx`
cycle goes with them.

### 2. The bounds (`core/num.ts`, `tools/eslint-rules`, thirty-one sites)

`atMost(value, limit, because)` and `atLeast(value, limit, because)` in `core/num.ts`, where the
third argument is the REASON — "what it holds", "what the corridor pays", "the hours there are" —
and `no-bounds` forbids the bare comparison-ternary shape as well as `Math.min`/`Math.max`. Every
bound in the engine is then a call with a stated reason, greppable and readable, and a bound with a
weak reason is visible as one. The law asks for the compensating mechanism; naming it at the site is
the cheapest enforcement of that.

### 3. The dust Law 8 retired (`register/`, `ledger/settlement.ts`, `audit/families/money.ts`)

Delete `deliveryDust` and every use of it; delete the lot-clearing branch; `deliverable` becomes
`qty <= free`; `moneyDelta`'s guard and its `allowNegative` parameter go together (the netting
question is genuinely settlement's — leg order inside one instruction must not decide an overdraft);
`precheck`'s money tolerance and `validateCellSide`'s comparison become exact; the money family's
B3.c check compares against zero. About forty lines, and the one branch that can destroy units goes
with them.

### 4. The guards the stores were supposed to have

`restate` invalidates; `stateEquity` takes the period and cycle it is called in (every caller has
them); the three rounding doors return `asQty(...)`; `splitOnTick` throws where it breaks; `forget`
guards; the registry validates derivative kinds and `tickShift` at assembly; `months` takes a
`positiveCount`. Then the `Qty` diff: change the six field types on the wire plus `Lot.qty` and
`Instrument.issued` and follow the compiler. It is large and mechanical and it is the difference
between the rule being enforced and being described.

### 5. The audit that tells the truth

`built` means every contribution is built; `unbuilt` is reported beside `contributions` with the
names, so a family carrying a stub says so. The flows family exempts the PAIR, not the party: the
split event already names `from`, `to` and `members`, so the exemption is those two cells' shared
instruments for that one event, and an entry or a death is checked normally by scaling `before` by
the weight ratio the event records — which is what the split restatement beside it already does.
Money's C4.c runs in the first period against the seed. The constructor throws `InvalidRegistry`
with a citation.

### 6. The declarations, the readers and the seams

One reader of staleness (`printOrThrow` is the right rule inside a period whose phases are ordered);
`place` deleted in favour of `periodOf`; `valueOfLots` accumulates through `sum`.
`loan.operatingCost` re-declared `kind: 'placeholder'` with
`standsInFor: { mechanism: "Banks Lending C1.d — the credit officer's hours", worklistItem: '13d' }`.
`namesAnItem` runs on every kind, not only `shape`. `ParamDecl.unit` becomes a small closed
vocabulary with reads that name what they expect (`params.periods(id)`, `params.ratio(id)`) — a
mechanical change across every read site, and the difference between a declared unit and a commented
one. `cellKey` is read by `Parties.add` and `CellKey` becomes
`Readonly<Record<CellKeyDimension, string>>`, **or** it is deleted and the key is stated as fixed;
leaving it is worse than either, because it tells the next person a change is cheap that is not.

The four seams: one `Brand<string, 'ModuleKey'>` a module re-brands for itself; `admits`/`marginLegs`
move into the market kind's own row; `MarketDecl` becomes a discriminated union on `kind` (both
throws go, `delivers` becomes a field); `DerivativeKindProfile.orders` moves onto a class module the
layer collects at assembly — same dispatch, same "one face", and `registry/` goes back to being data.
`SeedContext` exposes `Pick<>`s and the register's reads facade.

### One question to settle, and it is the owner's

`resolveMarketOrders` resolves a market buy to the HIGHEST ask and a market sell to the LOWEST bid —
"the worst level the other side actually posted". Seventeen sites post market orders, and the
consequential one is `central-bank-omo`: a central bank closing a gap towards its 25%-of-line target
is then a price-insensitive bidder at the top of the book in every sovereign session where it has
one. The quantity limit is real policy (Central Bank C1) and the module correctly refuses to stand in
any other market, so this is not Appendix B's buyer of last resort — but it makes the central bank
the marginal price-setter in the sovereign book rather than a large participant in it, which is more
aggressive than any real open-market operation. State the rule deliberately, either way.
