# Item 12b.1 — A price has a smallest piece

**The owner's decision, taken 2026-09-11.** Every quantity in this world has a realistic minimum
subunit, and a price is one of them: a fixed subdivision, declared like the others, not a real number
that happens to be a price. It is not on this list because a measurement asked for it — it is a
statement about what this world is made of, and it settles the question `docs/BUGS.md` 12b-1 raised.

**The finding it settles (12b-1).** `PricePrint.price` is a bare `number`; a fill is struck `at:
g.o.price`, whatever the poster computed; and so prints carry sixteen significant figures —
`share.etf.us` at `511.12891311042875`, `equity.firm.11` at `49.7938868935811`, `jgb.2036-03-15` at
`0.8887345371057928`. Every `units x price` read inherits it: a balance sheet at `15901695062647.4`,
an equity account at `2407881467552.351`, a revaluation delta at `-6097242077.790232`. A money
BALANCE is a whole count of pieces (`money:ecb:EUR 306717802678`) because a quantity has a type and
five doors (`core/tick.ts`); a price had neither.

**Why it is the same argument twice.** `core/tick.ts` argues the quantity grid from the real
mechanism — "a unit of anything real has a smallest piece and nothing finer exists" — and every word
of it is true of a price. A market quotes in cents, in pips, in thirty-seconds; it does not quote in
sixteen significant figures, and a level finer than its tick is not a level anyone can hit.
`clearing/market.ts` already names what the world lives with instead: cash lands on its own grain, so
"the rate a trade REALISES can differ from the print by less than one piece".

**Read first.** §3 Clearing C3, C4.c, D4; §2 Register A1.c; §1 Money A2, C1; Law 1, Law 2 (RESOLUTION
and how it is tested), Law 6, Law 8. Code: `core/tick.ts` (the five doors and why they exist),
`registry/registry.ts` (`UnitDecl.perUnit`, `subdivision`, `pieces`, `priceOf`, `pieceShift`),
`registry/grid.ts`, `clearing/solver.ts` (a level is POSTED and never a bracket),
`clearing/market.ts` (`toGrain` on the cash legs), `prices/price-store.ts`.

## What the shape has to be

**A price in this engine is already a ratio of two grids.** `Registry.priceOf(ccy, unit, perNamedUnit)`
states it as money pieces per piece of the thing, so 400 USD the tonne is four hundredths of a cent
the gram. The tick therefore CANNOT be "one money piece per thing piece" — that is coarser than real
prices in one direction and finer in another. It is its own declared subdivision, stated the way a
person states it (cents a share, a pip, a thirty-second) and converted at the registry boundary
exactly as `priceOf` converts a named price.

**It is a RESOLUTION (Law 2), so it shifts with the others.** `pieceShift` moves every unit's
subdivision together so the same world can be declared finer and its path shown not to move; the
price grid joins that shift, and the invariance test is what says the number is a resolution rather
than a preference somebody tuned.

**It goes on the POSTING door and never on the print.** A cleared level is a posted level (Clearing
C4.c — "posted and never a bracket"), so if every order is posted on the grid every print is on the
grid by construction, and nothing rounds an outcome. Rounding the print instead would be a bound on
a cleared price, which is Law 6, and it would put a second writer on a number the solver already
decided (Law 4).

**Which way it rounds belongs to the poster.** A buyer's limit rounds one way and a seller's the
other, and the door makes the author say which — the same decision `downTick`/`upTick` already force
on a size, for the same reason: a kernel that picks silently has made a trading decision on somebody
else's behalf.

**Where it lands and why.** After 12b, before 12c. 12b is one wrong conversion and nothing to do with
resolution, so it finishes first. 12c rewrites the door where dealers post both sides of a quote, and
12d rewrites every test that reads a number — so the grid must be under both of them or each is built
twice. It cannot go earlier than now: the past is built and Law 13 does not undo it.

**Objective.** No price anywhere in the engine that is not a whole number of its market's ticks, with
the grid declared, shifted with the other subdivisions, and the invariance measured.
