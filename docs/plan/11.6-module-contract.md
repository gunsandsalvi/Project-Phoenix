# Item 11.6 — The module contract: a kind lives where its behaviour lives, and a participant says which markets it is in

**Read this part first.** Two findings from item 12, both of them about what a `SystemModule`
DECLARES, neither of them moving a single number. Item 2a is the precedent for taking them together:
it settled the module contract in one item because the contract is one subject, and these two are
the two places item 12 found it still incomplete. Nothing here changes a mechanism, a price, a
boundary or an economic decision — which is what makes it safe to work between 11.5 and 12a, where
every neighbouring item moves numbers (Law 18: layout and traversal are free; gate on behaviour).

**Objective.** Every party kind is declared by the one module that owns its behaviour, so the guard
that says "a module declaring a depositor must say how it leaves" can cover all of them; and a
market session asks for orders only from the participants that could have one, so the cost of a
period stops being the product of every market and every party.

**Read first.** `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` 4.9b ("a kind is owned by exactly one module", the
`SystemModule` contract, `ParticipantView`/`MechanismContext`/`SeedContext`); `docs/PLAN.md` §4.4
(an inserted kernel item carries a record entry) and its contract tables; §26 Banks Funding A1.d;
§3 Clearing A2, B1; Law 4, Law 15, Law 18. Code: `registry/profiles.ts` (`KERNEL_PARTY_KINDS`),
`world/assemble.ts` (`requireBankChoices` and the comment that records the gap), `world/world.ts`
(`runOne` and the participant fan-out), `mechanisms/firms/`, `mechanisms/households/`, every module
that imports `FIRM` or `HOUSEHOLD`.

## The findings this item carries

### The kernel declares two kinds whose behaviour a module owns (item 12's finding **12-7**; `docs/RECORD.md`)

**Where.** `registry/profiles.ts`, `KERNEL_PARTY_KINDS`: `FIRM` and `HOUSEHOLD`.

**How it was found.** Building the `bankChoices` door at item 12. The guard that belongs with it — a
module that declares a depositor must say how it leaves (Banks Funding A1.d) — can only ask a module
about its OWN declaration, because a world assembled from four modules to exercise one kernel door
has the kernel's party kinds in it and no `firms` to speak for them. So the guard covers `FUND` and
`FUND_MANAGER`, which the `funds` module declares, and says nothing about `FIRM` and `HOUSEHOLD`,
whose reasons live in `firms` and `households` while their kinds live in the kernel. A first attempt
that checked every registered kind refused six legitimate reduced worlds, which is the same defect
read from the other end.

**Why it is a defect and not a preference.** ARCHITECTURE 4.9b: the kernel "owns the money instrument
kind and the party kinds money needs". Money needs the central bank, the treasury and the bank — it
does not need a firm or a household.

**One thing was already fixed on the way and is worth naming**: `SMALL_BUSINESS` was a third such
kind and nothing anywhere ever instantiated it (13e declares its own `smallFirm`). It is deleted.

### A period costs the product of every market and every party (item 12's findings **12-12** and **12-11**'s residual; `docs/RECORD.md`)

**Measured.** `foundationWorld('full')` — 30 banks, 3,000 named firms, 30,000,000 people, 3,169
parties, 303 instruments, 261 markets. Assembly 0.4s; audit green in every built family.

```
        before item 12's layout work     after
p1      27.1s                            7.0s
p2      30.5s                            8.6s
p3      (not reached in 115s)           12.4s
```

What one period SAYS at p3: 69,344 events over 36,887 settled instructions — 18,407 revaluations,
16,804 settlements, 7,382 surprises, 5,971 perishings, 3,038 credit quotes, 3,000 plans. None of
that is a defect: three thousand firms deciding and being revalued is what three thousand firms cost.

**What is left, and it is this item's.** Four fifths of the remaining cost is the market fan-out: a
session asks every party of a kind whether it has an order in it, so 261 markets against 3,169
parties is 827,000 questions a period whose answer is almost always no. **The kernel cannot guess
which parties could have an order** — that is a mechanism's own business — so the fix is a contract
change: a participant declares which markets it is ever in, the way `ParticipantDecl.in` already
declares which SORT of market it answers for (item 12 added that door for exactly this reason on the
other axis). Same numbers, less traversal.

**And what is NOT this item's.** Events per period keep RISING with the run (3,929 at p1 to 21,000
at p5 in one 30-bank run) because rows accumulate and each says something every period. That is a
real growth in what the world has to say, not a defect — but at thirty million people and three
thousand firms it decides whether a long run is possible at all, and whether it is material is a
measurement (worklist 16, Law 11). Item 12's traversal work is already done and is not to be redone:
the journal and ledger keep a list per kind, a list per period and the latest per (kind, subject),
which took 30 banks / 300 firms from 5.7s to 1.5s at p3 and 10.5s to 2.2s at p4.

## Exit criteria

No party kind is declared by a module that does not own its behaviour, and the guard that says so
has no exception list. A market session asks a question only of a party that could answer it, and
the world it produces is identical instruction for instruction.

## Guard

Law 18 (mechanisms, economics and boundaries never change in performance work; gate on behaviour);
Law 15 (data in a registry, behaviour in a profile); Law 4 (one writer per fact); Law 16 (a stale
comment is a defect — the `assemble.ts` note goes with the fix it describes).
