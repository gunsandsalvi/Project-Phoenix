# 10.3 — A quantity is a whole number of indivisible pieces

**Status: in flight.** The engine is converted and typechecks; 25 of 269 engine tests are still red
and every one of them is listed below. Nothing here is speculative: the numbers are from a full run
of `npx vitest run` in `packages/engine` at the last commit on this branch.

## Why this exists

Item 10.2 gave every unit a smallest piece and made it a power of two — 2^-20 of a PHX, about a
ten-millionth — so that whole pieces would add exactly in binary. That bought exact arithmetic and
paid for it with a granularity nothing real has. The owner's finding, and it is correct: **money
exists up to the cent, nothing below a cent can be lent, paid or refused, and there is no dust
anywhere.** A grid of 2^-20 does not say that. It also produced a live defect — a bank was recorded
as refused funding for a shortfall of 1.8e-7, that refusal is a public observable, and every
uninsured depositor left it (fixed at the cause; see the money-market notes below).

A decimal cent and a floating-point fraction cannot both be exact: `0.07 + 0.01` is not `0.08` in
binary. The only way to have both a realistic piece and no dust is the way every real ledger does
it: **the state holds a COUNT of pieces and the count is an integer.** Integers add, subtract and
compare exactly up to 2^53, so a balance moved a million times is exactly the balance and no check
needs a tolerance at all.

## The shape

- `UnitDecl.perUnit` — how many indivisible pieces one NAMED unit is divided into. `registry/grid.ts`
  holds the four numbers: money 100 (the cent), a tonne 1,000,000 (the gram), a whole thing 1 (a
  machine, a share), an hour 1 (labour is contracted by the hour and no wage is struck for part of
  one).
- `Registry.subdivision(unit)`, `.pieces(unit, named)`, `.named(unit, pieces)`, `.priceOf(ccy, unit,
  perNamedUnit)` — the ONLY boundary between a person's number and the state's. A seed says
  `cash(ctx, 100_000)` and 10,000,000 cents go into the register; a price of 400 PHX the tonne
  becomes four hundredths of a cent the gram. Nothing downstream converts anything, because
  everything downstream is already a count.
- `core/tick.ts` — `onTick` is now "a safe integer"; `toTick/downTick/upTick` round to whole pieces;
  `splitOnTick` and `commonGrain` work in counts; `downToGrain/toGrain` round to what two parties can
  exchange (the lcm of their weights) in ONE rounding, never two.
- `resolution.pieceShift` — Law 2's knob, a multiplier on every unit's subdivision. Declared once,
  and the seed's own conversions carry it, so the same world can be declared in tenths of a cent.
- **The world is redenominated ×1000** so that a cent is a sensible piece of it: a weekly wage is
  ~940 PHX where it was ~0.65, a firm opens with 25,000–125,000 PHX, grain is 400 PHX the tonne.
  Before this the seed denominated a person's week at two-thirds of a currency unit, at which scale a
  cent is a fifth of a week's pay. The owner's rule applies: if the world changes when the amounts
  are made realistic, the world was wrong.
- `test/units.ts` — `phx()`, `tonnes()`, `perTonne()`, `perHour()`, `machinesPerTonne()` so a test
  says what it means instead of writing a bare number in the wrong unit.
- `test/expected.ts: paidTo(w, party, cause)` — what actually reached a party, read off the wire.
  Needed everywhere, because a balance now also moves by the week of deposit interest its bank pays
  it, which is the funding market's doing and not the mechanism under test.

## What is exact now, and measured

`test/tick.test.ts` runs the whole foundation world at three subdivisions — the cent, a tenth of a
cent, a hundredth of a cent — and asserts:

- **every structural invariant holds EXACTLY at all three** (money conserved, holdings sum to issued,
  no residual anywhere): `reds` is 0, not "within" anything;
- **real output is the same** in tonnes at all three, to within one coarse piece per batch started
  (a bound derived from the arithmetic, not a band anybody chose);
- the wire throws on half a cent and settles a whole one.

## The 25 tests still red

Each line is a real thing to look at, not a number to nudge. Grouped by what is actually wrong.

### Test-side units not yet converted (mechanical)

- `accrued.test.ts` ×2 — a trade in the middle of a coupon period; the world's own numbers.
- `auction.test.ts` ×2 — `the obligation`: no dealer bids (their limits are in pieces now, and the
  auction sizes are not); `when the dealers step back`: expects the tail to be exactly 0.
- `dealers.test.ts` ×2 — `shrinks the bid to whichever of its limits binds` now says `book` where the
  test expects `money`, because the desk's cash and its limits were rescaled by different amounts;
  `shows inventory…` reads a negative book.
- `doors.test.ts` ×1 — `expected 6501256 to be less than 6500020`: a balance assertion that needs
  `paidTo(w, party, 'coupon')` taken out of it (deposit interest), same as goods/loans/credit-events.
- `equity.test.ts` ×2, `etf.test.ts` ×3, `funds.test.ts` ×1 — share counts and share prices. The
  share is now indivisible and its opening level is `MONEY_PIECES` (one PHX); the seed's share counts
  were NOT scaled with it, so a household member holds a fraction of a share and rounds to none. This
  is the one conversion I did not finish: **scale the seeded share counts ×1000** (desks' `opens`,
  and whatever the funds subscribe at) or lower the opening level, and the ETF/dividend/NAV tests
  come back with it.
- `households.test.ts` ×2 — `taxes what a household was actually paid` (no tax collected: the
  threshold is in pieces); `moves cells across a threshold` (24000 vs 12000, a weight test whose
  amounts are money).
- `market-primary.test.ts` ×1, `treasury.test.ts` ×1 (`employs people on rows`) — auction and wage
  sizes.
- `world.test.ts` ×1 — `routes reserves between banks and none within one`.

### Findings about the world, not about units

- **`capital.test.ts` — `a dearer cost of capital means a dearer quote, less investment and less
  output` (XI-4).** The dear world invests MORE than the cheap one (158.5M vs 145.4M). This was
  failing before this change and before the money market: it is a finding about the investment
  decision or about what a dearer cost of capital reaches, and it is the one XI-4 asserts as a chain.
  Law 11: the misbehaving number is not the work item; find the missing mechanism.
- **`treasury.test.ts` — `funds itself over a year`: 14 failed settlements.** The state cannot pay
  something 14 times in a year. Its transfers and purchases are now real money at the new scale;
  whether this is the funding constraint working (XI-9) or the scale being wrong is the question.
- **`omo.test.ts` ×3.** The central bank's income changed sign: with the corridor it PAYS the floor
  on every reserve parked with it overnight (Money Market C1.a — the cash leaves the banking system)
  and earns coupons on a smaller book. `buys towards the share policy chose` misses its target and
  `remittance` hands over nothing. §31 E3/E4 allow a loss, so the tests' expectations are probably
  what is wrong — but the number wants deriving before it is written down.
- **`world.test.ts` — `runs a year of the whole chain, and every family it has built is green`.**
  See item 11's own list: a bank still dies eventually and there is no bank resolution.
- **`tick.test.ts` — `its money converges`.** Real output is exactly invariant across subdivisions;
  the money stock is not monotonically converging over 8 periods (78.76 vs 1.7e-5). Its decisions are
  thresholds, so two grids diverge once one of them crosses one. The assertion as written is too
  strong for 8 periods; either measure at 2–3 periods, or state the claim as the exactness test above
  already does and drop the convergence one.

## Lint (10 errors)

- Seven unused imports left by the conversion: `currencyUnit` in `clearing/market.ts`,
  `mechanisms/dealers/index.ts`, `mechanisms/households/index.ts`, `mechanisms/money-market/index.ts`
  (also `asPeriod`, `announced` there), and `phx` in `test/labour.test.ts`.
- Three `phoenix/no-magic-numbers`: `money-market/index.ts:613` (`100 * MONEY_PIECES`),
  `treasury/index.ts:234` (`12 * MONEY_PIECES`) and `:250` (`30_000 * MONEY_PIECES`). **This is a
  real design question, not a lint nit.** A param denominated in money now needs its named amount
  multiplied by the subdivision at the declaration site, and the rule forbids the literal. Either
  give `ParamDecl` the unit it is denominated in and let the register convert (which also makes those
  params scale with `pieceShift`, which they currently do not), or move the amounts into
  `registry/`. The second is cheaper; the first is right.
