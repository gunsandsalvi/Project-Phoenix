# 10.3 — A quantity is a whole number of indivisible pieces

**Status: the conversion is done and the world is green but for four tests, and all four of them are
one missing mechanism.** Lint, typecheck and the spec citations pass; 270 of 274 engine tests pass.
Nothing below is speculative: the numbers are from `npm run check` at the last commit on this branch.

## Why this exists

Item 10.2 gave every unit a smallest piece and made it a power of two — 2^-20 of a PHX, about a
ten-millionth — so that whole pieces would add exactly in binary. That bought exact arithmetic and
paid for it with a granularity nothing real has. The owner's finding, and it is correct: **money
exists up to the cent, nothing below a cent can be lent, paid or refused, and there is no dust
anywhere.** A grid of 2^-20 does not say that. It also produced a live defect — a bank was recorded
as refused funding for a shortfall of 1.8e-7, that refusal is a public observable, and every
uninsured depositor left it.

A decimal cent and a floating-point fraction cannot both be exact: `0.07 + 0.01` is not `0.08` in
binary. The only way to have both a realistic piece and no dust is the way every real ledger does
it: **the state holds a COUNT of pieces and the count is an integer.** Integers add, subtract and
compare exactly up to 2^53, so a balance moved a million times is exactly the balance and no check
needs a tolerance at all.

## The shape

- `UnitDecl.perUnit` — how many indivisible pieces one NAMED unit is divided into. `registry/grid.ts`
  holds the four numbers: money 100 (the cent), a tonne 1,000,000 (the gram), a whole thing 1 (a
  machine, a share), an hour 1 (labour is contracted by the hour and no wage is struck for part of
  one).
- `Registry.subdivision(unit)`, `.pieces(unit, named)`, `.named(unit, pieces)`, `.priceOf(ccy, unit,
perNamedUnit)` — the ONLY boundary between a person's number and the state's.
- `core/tick.ts` — `onTick` is "a safe integer"; `toTick/downTick/upTick` round to whole pieces;
  `splitOnTick` and `commonGrain` work in counts; `downToGrain/toGrain` round to what two parties can
  exchange in ONE rounding, never two.
- `ParamDecl.denominated` + `params.amount(id, unit)` — **a parameter that is an AMOUNT** is declared
  the way a person says it (thirty thousand PHX, seven thousand hours, four hundred thousand units of
  a line) and the register hands back the count of pieces. This was the design question 10.2 left
  open, and it is answered the expensive way rather than the cheap one because it is the only way a
  declared amount MOVES WITH `pieceShift`. It also deleted the three `no-magic-numbers` lint errors,
  which were the symptom that pointed at it.
- `resolution.pieceShift` — Law 2's knob, a multiplier on every unit's subdivision. The registry is
  built on it, so the parameter register is built twice out of one list of declarations: once with no
  units (which can answer only `pieceShift`) and once against the registry that number built.
- **The world is redenominated ×1000** so that a cent is a sensible piece of it: a weekly wage is
  ~940 PHX where it was ~0.65, a firm opens with 25,000–125,000 PHX, grain is 400 PHX the tonne.
- `test/units.ts` — `phx()`, `par()`, `tonnes()`, `perTonne()`, `perHour()` so a test says what it
  means instead of writing a bare number in the wrong unit.
- `test/expected.ts` — `paidTheSame`/`sameQuantity` now say "to within N PIECES", which is the number
  N, because a piece IS the number. `paidTo(w, party, cause)` reads what actually reached a party off
  the wire, because a balance now also moves by the week of deposit interest its bank pays it.
- `Snapshot.subdivisions` — the surface reads back in named units (`packages/app/src/ui/render.ts`),
  and nothing is converted on the way out of the engine.

## What this change FOUND

- **The desks were a hundred times too small.** `mechanisms/dealers/data.ts` states named PHX
  (`cash: 400_000`, `limitAggregate: 1_200_000`) and 10.2 had converted them as though they were
  already pieces, so desk A opened with 4,000 PHX against its bank's 400,000. Fixed at the boundary
  (`registry.pieces` in the seed, `params.amount` for both limits), which restores exactly the
  proportion the world had before 10.2.
- **A desk was a footloose wholesale depositor.** Once the desks were the right size, desk A was
  essentially the whole of bank A's corporate funding — and `moveDeposits` moved it to bank B for two
  basis points, killing bank A. Dealer Desks A1 says a desk IS its bank's trading arm and its account
  is there because that is what it is. Now a party kind says whether it `choosesBank` (Law 15: the
  profile answers, nothing branches on a kind), and a bank, a treasury, an estate and a desk do not.
- **The ETF was a rounding error with a manager attached.** Its launch was 60 shares against an
  equity line of 120,000; the share counts had been scaled ×1000 with the redenomination and its had
  not.
- **The named red is gone.** Every foundation world now runs a full year with ZERO violations in
  every family. Until this change each of them reported one every auction cycle — a bank overdrawn at
  the central bank with nobody able to lend it the difference overnight (Money B3.b) — and
  `test/expected.ts` existed to name and hide exactly that. The corridor (item 11's built part) is
  what a bank goes to instead, so the exemption is deleted rather than widened: `unexpected()` is now
  every violation the audit reported.
- **A fund can be gated for ever on a fraction of a share.** `households/portfolio.ts:fundOrders`
  asks for `short / perShare` shares per member, which is not a whole number; the fund pays the whole
  shares it can and the remainder sits on its book for ever, so a fund that owes nobody anything
  reports a gate every period. **Named at the site and NOT fixed here** — see below.

## The four tests still red, and why they are all one thing

**All four end the same way: a bank fails, and this world has nowhere to put it.** That is the
blocker item 11 already names (its plan file, "BANK RESOLUTION (140-144) — this is the blocker"), and
it is not something 10.3 may build (Law 10). What a dead bank leaves behind is visible in the audit —
`money:bank.a:PHX is money issued by a non-issuer`, `bank.b has ceased but still holds
gov.north.2031-03-15`, an estate whose assets and liabilities do not meet its equity account, a loan
outstanding whose lender of record holds nothing — and in the wire as a `Forbidden [Money E4]` when a
mechanism addresses the dead party directly.

- **`capital.test.ts` — `a dearer cost of capital means a dearer quote, less investment and less
output` (XI-4).** With both banks needing 150% on their own capital, bank B fails at period 10 and
  the run goes red. Underneath it there is a second finding, seen while the red above was hidden: the
  dear world invested MORE than the cheap one (158.5M against 145.4M), which is the chain XI-4
  asserts running backwards. It cannot be looked at again until the bank survives long enough to be
  measured, and when it can be, Law 11 applies: the misbehaving number is not the work item.
- **`funds.test.ts` — `sells into a market it does not price when the buffer runs out`.** The gate
  never empties, because of the fractional-share request named above; the run continues and bank B
  dies at period 30. Rounding the request at its cause is the right fix and it was tried: it takes
  the money fund's forced sale past what this world can absorb — the bank funded against the bills it
  dumps fails at period 22 — so it belongs in the same change as the resolution (Banks Capital D,
  XI-2).
- **`funds.test.ts` — `a year with a redemption wave in it`.** Every household redeems at period 20;
  the fund sells its whole book into the bill market; bank B cannot roll a repo at period 22.
- **`omo.test.ts` — `lets the book run off when reinvestment is off`.** With reinvestment off the
  base shrinks under the banks, and by period 5 bank A is overdrawn at the central bank on the
  **unpriced** path (`recordedAs: 'reserveOverdraft'`) and the money market refuses it. Item 11's own
  list has "prices the reserve overdraft; deletes the recorded-but-unpriced overdraft path" — this is
  that path, still there. The test's own claim is separately suspect: the world with reinvestment OFF
  holds MORE money than the one with it on, because those unpriced overdrafts create central-bank
  money without a lender row.

**So this item is done except for its record, and it cannot write one until item 11 builds bank
resolution.** The two are not separable by any ordering: 10.3's conversion is what made bank failure
reachable in the first place, by putting the desks back at the size their own data states. The
worklist row says so.

## Other findings, recorded and not acted on

- **A yield solve throws past its bracket.** `Impossible [Law 7] yield of gov.north.bill.2027-03-15:
0.27 is outside [0.427, 1]` at period 61 of a long run. Nothing in the worklist runs that far yet;
  it is a real defect in the price-to-yield inversion and it will stop item 16 dead.
- **The auction announces a size that is not a whole number of pieces.** `treasury/index.ts:announce`
  offers `size / reservation` units of par. It reaches the wire through `downToGrain`, so nothing
  off-grid is ever settled, but an issuer offering 53,308,760.87 units of par is announcing a
  quantity that does not exist.
- **A desk pays rent to the bank its DATA names, not the one the party register says it banks at**
  (fixed here: `payRent` reads the register), and when that bank has ceased there is nobody to pay —
  which is the same hole again.
