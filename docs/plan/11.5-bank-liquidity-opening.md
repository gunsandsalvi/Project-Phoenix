# Item 11.5 — The banking system opens meeting its own liquidity standard

**Read this part first.** Item 11 established the pattern for one half of a bank's opening sheet: **a
bank opens where its own capital rule puts it**, derived, so that it neither has to shrink on the
first morning nor opens with headroom nobody gave it. Its LIQUIDITY never got the same treatment,
and what follows is what that costs, measured at item 12's close and repeated here in full so this
file stands on its own.

**The finding.** A bank quotes a borrower only if it has room, and room is the least of capital,
appetite and FUNDING. Funding room is `liquid − couldLeave`: what its reserves and its own
unencumbered paper would raise at its central bank's window (Money Market C1, C1.a), less the
uninsured deposits that could run. Every bank opens with that negative:

```
bank.a  reserves 18.1e9  paper 25.0e9  liquid 64.8e9  couldLeave 71.1e9  → room −6.3e9
bank.b  reserves 10.1e9  paper 19.6e9  liquid 29.8e9  couldLeave 40.7e9  → room −10.9e9
```

**Demonstrated to be the gate.** With deposits fully insured — `regulation.depositInsurance.limit`
raised so that nothing can run, and nothing else touched — credit quotes go from 23 to **230** over
the same ten periods.

**What it costs.** Everything downstream of credit: no loans, so no firm has a quoted rate, so
`costOfCapital` is Missing for most of them, so `project()` is never reached and no firm ever
commissions plant however plant-bound it is. That is most of `capital.test.ts` and much of
`loans.test.ts` and `credit-events.test.ts`.

**It is the opening, not the rule.** `couldLeave` is a real stress (the uninsured part of every
deposit) and `liquid` is a real read. What is wrong is that the banks open holding about two thirds
of their assets in government paper, which the window takes only at a haircut, and one third in
reserves — below the standard they are then measured against.

**Where it lands and why.** Before 12a. The size of the central bank's balance sheet is what decides
the mix — its paper IS the banks' reserves — and everything after this that wants a firm to borrow,
12a's income statement above all, reads a world where nobody does until it is fixed.

## The other findings this item carries

### A bank fails in period 7 of the foundation world (item 12's finding **12-5**; `docs/RECORD.md`)

**Measured.** `bank.b` ceases in period 7 with `bank.a` as its successor. Its equity walked up
28.7M → 31.2M over periods 1–6 and then went to zero in one step, which is the resolution taking the
book rather than a loss landing on zero. Ahead of it: `moneyMarket.refused` for `bank.b` at periods
5, 6 and 7 (short 1, then 45,554,802, then 45,194,801, reserves −23,288,853 at period 6), then
`centralBank.refused` with `collateral: 0, solvent: true` — refused at the window with nothing left
to pledge while still solvent. That is Banks Funding D6 firing: a funding failure, distinct from
insolvency, and the audit is clean through all of it.

**Why it is this item's.** It appeared when the opening balance sheet stopped stating three reserve
figures and started deriving them from the central bank's own sheet, and it is the same sentence as
the finding above read at period 7 instead of period 0: a bank that opens below the standard runs its
account down and then cannot fund itself. Whether it is a bank that SHOULD fail — the mechanism
working — or a seed that funds it wrongly is exactly what deriving the opening decides.

**And it is not one seed's accident.** Nine worlds — two banks, three and four, each at one, two and
four cells per key — run 26 periods with every audit family at zero and the population EXACTLY 6,000
in all nine, which is the invariance the count-of-banks step wanted. What is not invariant is how
many banks are left:

| banks opened with | grain 1 | grain 2 | grain 4 |
| ----------------- | ------- | ------- | ------- |
| 2                 | 2 alive | 2       | 2       |
| 3                 | 1       | 2       | 1       |
| 4                 | 2       | 1       | 1       |

and what the survivors hold liquid against what could leave them swings from 181M/109M to 459M/400M
across the nine. **A world that opens with more banks ends with fewer** — because the more banks
there are, the thinner each one's reserves are when it opens. Nothing here is tuned and nothing is
judged: whether the swing is material is Part XII's question at worklist 16 (Law 11). What this item
owes it is that the opening is a derivation, so the swing is whatever the draw makes it and not what
a stated share left over.

### A ceased bank keeps its reserve overdraft, and nobody is owed it (item 12's finding **12-10**; `docs/RECORD.md`)

**Measured.** `foundationSpec` at 30 banks / 300 firms, seed `probe`, periods 3–5:

```
p3 money(4): bank.ab closes -4,075,747,840 per member on money:cb.north:PHX with no lender row behind it
   names(2): bank.w has ceased and still holds -37,303,027 of money:cb.north:PHX
p4 money(3), names(6): bank.ab -2,982,351,011 and bank.z -817,328,529, both ceased
p5 the same two, unchanged
```

(Measured before item 12 renamed the countries: `cb.north`/`PHX` is today's `cb.us`/`USD`. The
strings are left as they were printed — a measurement is what it said.)

Three banks fail and each leaves a NEGATIVE balance on the central bank's money behind it. Two
families see it and say different halves of the same thing: `money` says an overdraft with no lender
row behind it (a borrowing with no lender is money with no issuer), and `names` says a party that has
ceased still holds something.

**What it is not.** Not the resolution missing the overdraft while it runs: the number does not move
after the bank ceases, so nothing is still running. It is that resolution ends a bank whose reserve
account is overdrawn and **the overdraft goes nowhere** — Appendix B's "no death without a
destination", applied to a liability rather than to a holding. `mechanisms/money-market/resolution.ts`
is where a failed bank's book is moved.

**Why it is this item's.** It needs a bank to fail, which is what a world of many banks does, and
this item cannot show a green audit over one while it stands.

### A bank defends a deposit past what the guarantee on it costs (item 12's finding **12-8**; `docs/RECORD.md`)

**Where.** `mechanisms/banks/treasury.ts`, `defended()`, read with `setBoard()`. Seen in
`money-market.test.ts` ("pays a deposit rate per class, and the classes are not one rate"): one bank
shows retail 0.0180 and wholesale 0.0130.

**What is wrong.** `setBoard` prices a class at `worth - margin - premium`, so an insured class is
offered less by exactly what the guarantee costs the bank — correct. `defended` then lets it MATCH a
rival at anything up to `worth`, and it compares the RATE against `worth` while the bank's cost of
that money is `rate + premium`. So a bank defending retail against a keen rival pays up to
`worth + premium` all-in for money worth `worth` — the thing `defended`'s own comment says it will
not do: "it stops at what the money is worth to it — past that it funds itself in the market
instead". The stopping point for a class is `worth` less what the guarantee on that class costs.

**It is a cause with one fix** (Law 12): one number, in one place, per class. It is NOT a bound — it
is the alternative D1 names, measured correctly for the class. It is this item's because what a bank
pays for insured money is what decides how much of its book `couldLeave` counts.

### The resolution invariance does not converge (item 12's finding **pre12-1**; `docs/RECORD.md`)

**Where.** `test/tick.test.ts`, "converges as the piece gets finer, in what it made and in the money
it holds". **Measured:** 0.00928 against 0.00898, a miss of about three per cent. That is Law 2's
resolution invariance for the whole grid — declare the same world in finer pieces and its path must
not move.

**And at item 12's close it is no longer a miss — it is a THROW.** Both tests in the file now stop
with

```
Impossible: [Law 8] what moves on treasury.us's money:fed:USD is 711624048078206100,
            which is not a whole number of pieces
```

so at a finer grain some site divides and does not land on the grid, and the convergence is not even
reached to be measured. A contract violation names a mechanism (ARCHITECTURE §5), so this is the half
to root-cause first: the three per cent above is what the invariance said when it could still run.

**And the trace lands in this item's own code**, which is why it is here and not in 12d:

```
Register.onTheGrid    register/register.ts:276
Register.moneyDelta   register/register.ts:420
endowMoney            world/assemble.ts:194
seed                  seeds/foundation.ts:1048   ← the treasury's buffer
assemble              world/assemble.ts:98
```

`foundation.ts:1048` is `endowMoney(TREASURY_US, USD, cash(ctx, buffer))`, and `buffer` is
`centralBankAssets × treasuryBufferShare` — a SHARE of the central bank's balance sheet, taken
without saying which way the odd piece goes. The size of that balance sheet is the unknown this whole
item solves for, and every share taken out of it has the same question to answer (Law 8: a quantity
is a whole number of pieces, and splitting one gives the odd piece to a named claimant).

**The open question is this item's to answer.** Whether the opening balance sheets are what moved
either half. This item re-derives every one of them, so it is the cheapest place to re-measure: if
the throw and the miss go, it was the opening and the record says so; if either stays, it is a
finding about the grid, it becomes an item of its own at that point, and both measurements above are
its starting numbers.
