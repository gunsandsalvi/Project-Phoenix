# Item 11.3 — A depositor decides from its own balance, and the count of banks is a resolution

**Objective.** Two things item 11 found and 11.2's bank makes buildable, and one boundary 11.2 left
for here. (1) The switching decision
reads nothing the depositor has — `forRate = (rival − mine) > cost` compares class-level rates with a
class-level constant — so every member of a class crosses in one instant whatever the grain, and the
cell structure is decorative in it (XI-15: `Σ f(xᵢ)·wᵢ` with an `f` that ignores `xᵢ`). (2) With two
banks every deposit shift is 100% concentrated by construction, and no household grain changes that:
the count of NAMED institutions is a resolution of its own, never yet measured.

## Design

- **The decision belongs to the decider.** `moveDeposits` runs in the market module's
  `MechanismContext` and reads every depositor's balance to decide for it — a venue deciding for a
  party, with a view of private state no party may have (Observer A4), which is the defect 11.2 fixes
  for banks on the other side of the deposit. It moves: the households, firms and funds modules each
  decide where their parties bank, from that party's own `ParticipantView` of two public facts — the
  rate on the board (D5.a) and whether its bank looks in trouble (E2.a) — through the kernel's
  `moveBank` door. The deposit CLASS stops being a table the market keeps and becomes a declaration on
  the party-kind profile (`PartyKindProfile.depositClass`, beside `choosesBank`): the profile answers,
  nothing branches (Law 15). The market keeps what is the market's — the insured split (A1.a), what
  could leave (C2), the premium (D4).
- **The cost of moving is an AMOUNT** of the money the account is in — what a move costs a member to
  do — per class, kind `technology`, `denominated: true`. The decision, per cell and per member:
  `gain = (rival − mine) × per-member balance × one period; moves = gain > cost`. Small savers move
  only at a big gap; a large depositor moves for a few basis points; a class at one bank crosses at
  different gaps in different weeks because balances differ — heterogeneity of STATE doing the work a
  stated dispersion would fake. The treasury's unforced rate and its defence read the class's marginal
  member (largest per-member balance at this bank, F1) and `cost / balance` — one read, one rate per
  class (B2.b). The safety move (E4) is unchanged.
- **Four banks declared**, distinct preferences with a why each; `seed.banks` (RESOLUTION, default 2)
  opens the first N and spreads households, firms and the float across them. Every mechanism already
  iterates over banks; a test proves no code path names `bank.a` or `bank.b`.
- **`test/resolution/banks.test.ts`**: the same seed at 2, 3, 4 banks, stated the way `cells.test.ts`
  states its grains — population exact, audit at zero, the real side within what whole people do, the
  financial aggregates against what one whole bank crossing carries. If the aggregates move with the
  count, the finding is the resolution and it is recorded; nothing is tuned.
- **The liquidity target's size**: after 11.1 the treasury owns it as a decision. This item measures
  whether a target derived from the bank's own published liabilities holds both resolution
  invariances; if it does not, the size is a POLICY floor (`regulation.liquidityCoverage`) plus the
  bank's preference above it, with the reason — a regulatory ratio is a real primitive.
- `cells.test.ts` back to bounding the money the sectors hold by what whole people do, or the test
  says which bound is the honest one and why.
