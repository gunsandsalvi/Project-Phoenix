# Item 11.1 — The bank is one balance sheet with lines of business, and a depositor decides from its own balance

**Objective.** Three things item 11 got wrong at the root, and one it could not finish because of them.

1. **There is no trading desk.** A bank's dealing is a line of business of the bank — as its lending,
   its treasury and later its syndicate desk are — and it has no balance sheet, account, equity or
   death of its own. Item 9 made it a separate party (`desk.a`, `desk.b`), and every bug item 11 hit
   around a desk was that fact showing up somewhere else: a desk written down as a *creditor of its
   own bank* in a resolution; a trading book that consumed none of its bank's capital, so the
   weighted requirement asked the bank for nothing; a desk that walked to a rival for two basis
   points; a "rent" paid between two parties that are economically one; an XI-2 attempt that treated
   the bank's own arm as a large-exposure counterparty and emptied every book in the world. Dealer
   Desks A1 says "its own balance sheet **inside** a bank's" — a sub-ledger of one balance sheet, not
   a second one — and F2 says no desk is exempt from its own bank's capital and funding. With one
   balance sheet that FORBID cannot be broken.
2. **A depositor's decision reads nothing the depositor has.** `forRate = (rival − mine) > cost`
   compares two class-level rates with a class-level constant, so every member of a class crosses in
   the same instant whatever the grain, and the cell structure is decorative in that decision
   (XI-15: `Σ f(xᵢ)·wᵢ` with an `f` that ignores `xᵢ`). Moving an account costs an AMOUNT — an
   afternoon, a form, a payroll re-pointed — while the gain scales with the balance; so the threshold
   is `cost / balance`, different for every cell because the world made their balances differ, and
   a finer grain then smooths the response the way XI-15's measurement expects it to.
3. **Macro states cannot emerge from two banks.** With N=2 every deposit shift is 100% concentrated by
   construction — one bank's squeeze is the other's flood — and no household grain changes that. The
   count of NAMED institutions is a resolution of its own, and XI-15's measurement has only ever
   been run over cells.
4. **The last placeholder** (`bank.liquidityBuffer.perDeposit`) could not be deleted because any
   perturbation of what banks want of paper flipped a knife-edge event in a two-bank world with a
   one-block deposit market. It waits on 2 and 3, and on the trading book being where it belongs.

## Design

### 1. One bank, several lines

The `DESK` party kind, the two desk parties, their accounts, their equity accounts and their deaths
are deleted. The bank IS the dealer: the bank's own participant posts the two-way quote in every
market a line of its makes, out of the bank's own inventory, priced off the bank's own cost of funds
and the bank's own capital. `mechanisms/dealers/` folds into `bank-lending/` as `dealing.ts` (the
quote and the orders — `quote.ts` moves across almost unchanged, because it never read anything but
the desk's own state) and `lines.ts` (the lines and their data). The module id stays `bank-lending`
for this item; renaming it `banks` is a separate one-line change once every `requires` and test
filter has been touched for other reasons.

A **line** is data on the bank row (Law 15): which instrument kinds it makes a market in, and the
bank's own concentration preference. This world's banks each have three lines from this item on —
`lending`, `dealing`, `treasury` (the funding and liquidity side, which is what the money market
already talks to) — and the syndicate/IBD line arrives with 13f. The dealing line makes markets in
**equity shares and fund shares**. It does NOT make sovereign paper: the bank's treasury line already
quotes both sides of every sovereign line out of its liquidity book (`sovereign-curve`'s bank
participant, at its required yield ± the surplus premium), and two participants of one bank posting
into one book would be two reasons for one demand. What that costs is stated up front and measured
after: bill liquidity in a forced sale is then the treasury lines' bids at their required yields,
not a skewing dealer's — a real market maker's bid is exactly what item 9 put there, and this item
takes it out of the sovereign books deliberately, because a bank quoting its own liquidity buffer two
ways with a skew is a thing to build once the treasury line is its own decision (13f), not twice.

**Balance sheet consequences, each of which is a bug fixed:**

- **Inventory is the bank's holding.** The rent — a transfer between two parties that are one — is
  deleted, with its phase and its audit family. D3's "funding cost on the inventory, paid every period
  it holds it" is met more truly than before: the bank actually pays deposit interest and session
  rates on the liabilities that fund the inventory, every period, and the quote's carry reads that
  blended cost of funds (`bank.costOfFunds`, one writer) plus the capital charge. The desk's
  `requiredReturnOnCapital` was a second copy of the bank's `returnOnCapital` and goes.
- **The trading book consumes capital.** `capitalOf` weighs a holding of a kind the dealing line
  makes at `regulation.riskWeight.tradingBook` (already declared, never read until now), and
  everything else by the credit rule item 11 built (can the party behind the claim fail?). That is
  Basel's banking-book/trading-book split by INTENT, and intent here is the line's declared kinds —
  data. The weighted requirement stops asking a bank for nothing.
- **The dealing limit is a share of the bank's capital**, decided by the bank (below), not a stated
  money amount: `limitAggregate` and `limitPerInstrument` in money go; the concentration limit
  becomes a ratio of the dealing book (`bank.dealing.concentration.<bank>`, preference). So when the
  bank's capital falls, its dealing line shrinks — which is XI-2's third door done right: not a
  credit line cut to a counterparty, but a bank's own arm carrying less because the bank has less.
- **Resolution.** The dealing inventory is part of the failed book and moves to the acquirer with it.
  The sweep of "every party that banked at the dead bank" stays for the parties that do bank there.
- **The desk is not a depositor.** `desk` leaves the wholesale deposit class; `couldLeave` no longer
  counts a bank's own arm as money that could run from it.
- **Seed.** The banks open holding the equity float the desks opened holding (Seed A3: the float is
  the line, and somebody must hold it), and the desks' opening cash is gone — it was the bank's. The
  ETF is launched by the banks as authorised participants (`funds/data.ts`).

### 2. The bank allocates capital across its lines

A bank has one capital position (item 11's `bank.capital`, after the marks) and two lines that
consume it. Which line gets the headroom is the bank's DECISION, taken from its own record and not
from a share anybody stated:

- Each line's **return on the capital it used** is a read, per period, under the bank's own name and
  private (`bank.lines`): lending — interest received less losses booked, over the capital its book
  consumed at the credit weight; dealing — spread earned on the trades of its kinds less what the
  inventory did at the marks (F3: it can lose), over the capital the trading book consumed at the
  trading weight. Both numerators are reads of the wire and the revaluation journal (Law 19).
- Each period the bank **allocates the headroom it published**: the line with the higher realised
  return on capital over the bank's own memory (`credit.memory`, already its own) has first call on
  it up to what it asks — the dealing line asks for the book it wants to be able to carry, the
  lending line for the requests in front of it — and the other line takes what is left. A bank with
  no record yet has not decided (Appendix A): in period zero the lines take headroom in the order
  the period runs them, which is lending first, and the record says so.
- No line has a floor. A bank whose lending has lost money for a year puts its capital behind its
  dealing, and a bank whose inventory has bled puts it behind its loans. That is a bank deciding
  which of its businesses to be, which is what the user asked for and what a bank does.

Costs and headcount per line are NOT here: banks in this world employ nobody (labour is firms',
worklist 4) and pay no operating cost but the loan's `operatingCost`. A line's cost base arrives when
a bank has one (13d's labour mobility, 13f's financing lines), and the allocation above then reads
net of it without changing shape.

### 3. Information from the flow (Dealer Desks B2)

The dealing line faces named counterparties and the bank now owns what it saw. The smallest real
read of it, and the only one this item wires: a name that has been a NET SELLER to this bank's
dealing line over the bank's credit memory is a name that has been raising cash, and the lending
line's decision treats a request from it as it treats a name that published a shortfall — it is
asked why (the same door as Money B3.a's "it asked because it was short"). No number, no coefficient:
a decision that reads a fact the bank has and nobody else does. Everything richer than that (a
desk's view moving the bank's own PD of a name) needs the ratings layer's second opinion (XI-13, 12)
to stand beside it, and waits.

### 4. A depositor decides from its own balance

`deposit.switching.cost.<class>` becomes an AMOUNT of the money the account is in — what a move
costs a member to do — declared per class (retail: small, one person's afternoon; corporate: large,
an operation re-pointed; wholesale: small against the balances involved, which is A1.c's
rate-sensitivity as a consequence rather than a label) with kind `technology` and `denominated: true`.
The decision becomes, per cell and per member:

```
gain  = (rival's rate − my bank's rate) × my per-member balance × one period
moves = gain > cost
```

A cell of small savers moves only at a big gap; a large depositor moves for a few basis points; and
a class of depositors at one bank crosses at different gaps in different weeks because their balances
differ — which is heterogeneity of STATE, the thing the world produces, doing the work a stated
dispersion would have faked. The bank's own two decisions read the same fact once (Law 4): its
unforced rate and the level it defends a class at both use the class's **marginal member** at this
bank — the largest per-member balance in the class, read off its own deposit lines (F1) — and the
rate-equivalent of moving for that member, `cost / balance`. One rate per class still (B2.b); what
changed is that the bank knows who it is pricing to keep.

The safety move (E4) is unchanged: uninsured money leaves a bank that looks in trouble, and the
amount is what nobody insures.

### 5. The count of banks is a resolution

`money-market/data.ts` and `bank-lending/data.ts` declare FOUR banks with distinct preferences (two
more rows, each with a why); the seed opens the first `seed.banks` of them (a RESOLUTION parameter,
default 2 so every existing test is the same world) and spreads the household keys, the firms and
the float across the banks it opened. `test/resolution/banks.test.ts` runs the same seed at 2, 3 and
4 banks and states, the way `cells.test.ts` does, what moved and by how much — the population, the
audit at zero, the real side within what whole people do, and the financial aggregates, with the
bar derived from what a whole bank crossing carries. If the aggregates move with the count, the
finding is the resolution and it is written down; nothing is tuned.

### 6. The last placeholder

With the trading book inside the bank, the deposit market no longer a block, and the bank count a
knob: `bufferTarget` reads the bank's own published liabilities (item 11's first candidate, `base`)
and `bank.liquidityBuffer.perDeposit` is deleted. Both resolution tests (cells, banks) are the exit
gate. If it still trips them, the placeholder becomes a POLICY — `regulation.liquidityCoverage`,
a rule somebody wrote against a base the bank derives — with the reason in the record, because a
regulatory ratio is a real primitive and a placeholder is not.

### Parameters

Deleted: `desk.limit.perInstrument.*`, `desk.limit.aggregate.*`, `desk.requiredReturnOnCapital.*`,
the desks' opening cash, `deposit.switching.cost.*` as rates, `bank.liquidityBuffer.perDeposit`.
New: `bank.dealing.concentration.<bank>` (preference, ratio of the dealing book one line may be);
`deposit.switching.cost.<class>` (technology, an amount, denominated); `seed.banks` (resolution).
Read for the first time: `regulation.riskWeight.tradingBook` (policy, existed).

### Audit contributions

- `accounts`, contributor `bank-lending` (replaces `inventoryPaysRent`): **the trading book is
  capitalised** (F2) — every period, a bank's published risk-weighted assets are at least its
  holdings of the kinds its dealing line makes, at marks, at the trading weight. A bank whose book
  weighed less than that would be a desk exempt from its own bank's capital with a different name.
- `names` (existing, kernel): a `desk.*` party id anywhere is a failure of assembly, because no
  module declares the kind any more.

## Exit criteria

No party of kind desk exists and every market the desks made is still made two ways by a bank; a
bank's risk-weighted assets are not zero and which rule binds can go either way; a bank's dealing
book shrinks when its capital falls with no line to anybody cut; a rate gap moves part of a class,
not all of it, and the part is the large balances; a year runs green at 2, 3 and 4 banks; the
aggregates at three grains and three bank counts are stated against derived bars and nothing is
tuned; the engine has no placeholder, or has a policy where one was and says why.
