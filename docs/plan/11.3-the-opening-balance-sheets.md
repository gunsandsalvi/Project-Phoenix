# Item 11.3 — The opening balance sheets: a bank that is mostly its own capital, and reserves nobody issued

## The finding

Item 11.2 folded the dealer desks into the banks, and with the desks gone the banks became the only
market makers in sovereign paper. That put a number nobody had looked at on the front of every price
in this world, and it does not survive being looked at.

**A bank here is funded roughly three quarters by its own capital.** At period 12 of the foundation
world, `bank.a` publishes assets of 302M and capital of 218M against a deposit base of 67M. Its
blended cost of funds is what §24 B2 and XI-4 joint one say it is — a read across its whole mix
including capital — so it comes out near a tenth, because equity at the return its owners ask is the
dearest money it has and it has almost nothing else.

Everything downstream of that number is wrong in the same direction:

- **It cannot hold the paper the liquidity rule makes it hold.** What it requires of the sovereign
  (Corporate Credit E5: cost of funds, expected loss, capital charge) is around 10%, so a bill worth
  0.998 to a money fund is worth 0.963 to a bank.
- **It cannot win the auction it is obliged to bid at.** The obligation is discharged — it bids for
  half the size, every time — and the bid is three or four points under the issuer's reservation, so
  the auction is filled by whatever the money fund takes and the rest is withdrawn. The treasury
  places about an eighth of what it offers and misses eight of its own coupons in period 25 of the
  `tsy-g` world, which is a sovereign default caused by an arithmetic artefact.
- **There are two prices for one line.** The auction prints near par because the fund sets it; the
  secondary market prints at the banks' own reservation, three to twenty points lower. The curve is
  built from both and describes neither.

**And the central bank is born insolvent.** `SeedContext.endowMoney` issues the money of the party's
own bank; for a bank that bank is the CENTRAL bank, so every reserve the seed hands a bank is
central-bank money issued against no asset at all. The foundation seed hands out 70M of them. The
central bank then pays the floor rate on that balance for ever — the two banks park 100M-180M every
period — earns coupons on a portfolio a quarter of the size, and so records `centralBank.loss`
rather than a remittance in every year of every run. Its equity falls monotonically. 11.2 briefly
doubled this hole (to 135M) to keep the banks liquid after the desks' cash went; that half is
deleted, and what is left is the original.

**And the interbank market has nothing to do.** With the reserves the seed actually states, both
banks run a growing surplus, park it at the floor every period, and are never short — so the session
never clears, `moneyMarket.print` is empty, no bank is ever refused, no depositor ever sees a bank in
trouble, and the run, the contagion and the corridor's pass-through are all unreachable.

None of these is a number to adjust. They are one cause with two halves: **what the seed gives a
bank, and what it gives the central bank in exchange for it.**

## What an attempt at it found, and why it was thrown away

The first attempt rebuilt the opening balance sheets and got two of the four numbers it was after:
a bank opened at a quarter to an eighth of its own capital instead of three quarters, and its
blended cost of funds fell from near a tenth to between one and three points. **It was still thrown
away, and both reasons are worth more than the attempt was.**

**It was fitted.** The splits between the central bank, the three banks and the households, and the
size of the equity float, were chosen by running the world, reading the capital share off it and
going back to change them. That is a result wearing a preference's name (Law 2) and Part XII work
done in the middle of Part XIII (Law 11). Whatever this item states about an opening balance sheet
has to be ONE declared number with a reason — a share of what a bank holds that stands behind it —
with every endowment derived from it, not four numbers tuned until the answer looked right.

**It papered over an explosion with a bound.** With the balance sheets rebuilt, sovereign lines
walked away: a bill worth one went to nothing over a year, and when that was "fixed" it went to more
than everything it will ever pay, and the curve was asked for a yield that does not exist. What was
written to stop it was a floor under the offer and a cap over the bid — the dealer would not sell
below, or buy above, what keeping the paper was worth to it. **That is Law 6 exactly.** A lender's
reservation is a floor for a legitimate reason: parking at the central bank is a REAL ALTERNATIVE
TRANSACTION, at a rate, with a counterparty. "Keeping it" is not a transaction and has nobody on the
other side; it is a valuation, and taking the better of a quote and a valuation is a bound.

## The third cause, which is not the balance sheets

**A market whose only participants are dealers has no anchor.** Both sides of a dealer's quote come
out of its own view; its own view follows the last print; so when the only two or three parties in a
book are dealers sitting on the same side of their own targets, they cross each other in one
direction every period and walk the line away. Nothing in the model stops it and nothing should: the
missing thing is a party with a reason to be on the other side that is not another dealer's
inventory position. In this world the households hold a majority of the sovereign's debt and never
trade it, the money fund buys bills and nothing else, and the central bank buys to a share and
stops. That gap is why every version of this item exploded, and it is not the opening balance sheet.

## The fourth cause: a bank never distributes

Whatever share of its assets a bank opens with as capital, it retains every penny it earns — there
are no owners to pay (13g is where somebody comes to own a bank) — so the share ratchets up and its
cost of funds with it. An attempt that opened three banks at 27, 23 and 13 per cent saw one of them
at 14 per cent by the middle of a year and 9 by the end because its book grew faster; another would
have gone the other way. No opening state survives that on its own.

## What this item does

1. **A bank opens with a balance sheet, not a pile of assets.** Its capital is a stated share of what
   it holds (Seed A3 states the holdings and the share; the equity is still the read, Seed C1), and
   the rest of what it holds is funded by liabilities that exist: deposits somebody holds, and
   wholesale money somebody lent. Who holds them is the question Seed E1 answers today with
   "everybody opens with nothing", and that answer is what forces the whole balance sheet into
   equity — so this item decides which of the two gives.
2. **Nothing issues central-bank money against nothing.** Either the seed's reserves come with an
   asset on the central bank's side (paper it bought), or a bank opens with no reserves and gets
   them the way every later reserve arrives — through the wire. `endowMoney` to a party whose bank
   is a central bank is the one call that has to change or go.
3. **The interbank market has a reason to exist.** A world where no bank is ever short has no money
   market, and everything Money Market B, C and D describe is unreachable. Whether that follows from
   1 and 2 or needs its own cause is this item's first measurement.
4. **One price per line.** With a bank that can hold sovereign paper at a price near what a fund
   pays, the auction and the secondary market are the same market again, and the curve describes it.

## Where it sits

Ahead of the depositors' own decision and the count of banks (**11.4**), because that item measures
the liquidity target's size and the shift of a whole deposit class against the count of banks, and
both measurements are meaningless on a bank whose funding cost is an artefact of its own capital.

Behind **11.2**, because it is 11.2's fold that makes the number visible and 11.2's treasury that
gives a bank a liquidity target to be measured against.

## Steps

- [ ] The anchor: a party with a reason to hold sovereign paper on its own terms and to trade it — so a book whose dealers are all on one side has somebody on the other. Until this exists, every other step's world walks its own prices away and nothing downstream can be measured. Whether it is the households the seed already gives the paper to, the money fund widened past bills, or a party nobody has built yet is this item's first question
- [ ] The central bank's side: no reserve exists that it did not issue against something. `endowMoney` to a party whose bank is a central bank either takes the asset with it or goes; test: the central bank's equity at period zero is what its own holdings come to, and it does not fall by the seeded reserves
- [ ] The banks' side: a bank opens with liabilities as well as assets, so its capital is a share of what it holds rather than the whole of it; the sovereign's opening debt and who holds it are settled in the same change; test: a bank's published cost of funds is within the corridor's reach of what it actually pays its depositors, and its required yield on the sovereign is inside the range a fund will pay
- [ ] One price per line: the auction and the secondary market are the same market again, and the curve describes it; test: the ten-year point sits between what the two sides of the book will do, and an auction places what the issuer brings
- [ ] The interbank market has a reason to exist: measure whether 1 and 2 make a bank short often enough for Money Market B, C and D to be reachable, and if not, say what is missing; the twelve red checks listed above go green or the reason each does not is recorded

## Guard

Seed A3 (an endowment is an opening STATE, never an opening outcome); Seed C1 (equity is the read at
period zero and only events move it after); Money A1 (no money without an issuer, and no issuer
without the other side of it); Appendix B (no central-bank overdraft for the treasury — the same
prohibition read from the other side); Law 2 (a share of a balance sheet is a PREFERENCE, the size of
the sovereign's debt is a decision, and neither is a number chosen to make a check pass); Law 11 (the
misbehaving numbers above are findings, and this item is the missing mechanism they point at).
