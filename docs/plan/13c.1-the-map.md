# Item 13c.1 — The map

**Objective.** A drawn world with real geometry. A rectangular grid of tiles, each tile a declared
number of kilometres on a side; every tile is either ocean or belongs to exactly one **region**, and
regions are grouped into **countries**. A country — not a region — is what has a currency, a central
bank and a treasury, so a region becomes what it always should have been: a PLACE. The ground under
one region differs from the ground under the next. Regions that share a border get a land leg;
regions with a coast get ports and sea legs. Every leg's length in kilometres and its transit in
DAYS are read off the grid and the ground crossed, and both reach a carrier's cost — the hull is
used up per unit-kilometre, the crew is paid per day, and a hull on a long leg makes fewer round
trips per period, so it offers less. **No freight rate is declared anywhere**: distance sets a
carrier's COST and the session still sets the price (Law 3). And nothing anywhere caps what can be
built in a place: the ground gets worse as more of it is worked, which is what a rent is (Law 6).

**Read first.** §38 Freight A4, B1–B4, B2.a, C2, C3, D1–D6, D3.a, E1–E3 — built at 13c against four
legs declared identical, which is the defect this item exists to remove. §21 Commodities Spot A1,
A1.a, D1, D3 (location is part of identity; the same grade in two places is two instruments). §22
Goods B4 (the one-directional yield and its exponent). §39 Cross-Border A1, B4, D1–D6, F1, F2 and
§25 Indices D1 — every one of which says "region" and means a currency area. Seed B3; Currency B1.
Law 2 (a RESOLUTION is tested by invariance), Law 4, Law 6, Law 9, Law 15, Law 19; Appendix B (no
display-only number). Code: `registry/registry.ts` (`RegionDecl`), `registry/physical.ts` (`Recipe`,
`CapitalKindDecl`, `GoodTerms`), `registry/environment.ts`, `mechanisms/environment/data.ts`
(`drawClimate` — the pattern this item's draw follows), `mechanisms/freight/` (13c),
`mechanisms/firms/produce.ts` (the season's exponent), `seeds/foundation.ts` (`ABROAD`, `ROUTES`,
`CARRIER_COUNT`), `calendar/calendar.ts` (`startOf`), `core/tick.ts` (`upToTick`), `core/num.ts`
(`largest`), `rng/prng.ts`, `observer/observer.ts`, `packages/app/src/ui/render.ts`.

**Clauses this item meets.** Freight A4 (fully — routes become distinct because they are different
lengths over different ground), B2 and B2.a (capacity per hull falls with voyage length, so it
answers a shortage slowly for a reason), B4 (a leg's passability is read off the ground it crosses,
not declared per leg), D3, D3.a, D5 (a location basis with two real producing locations, reachable
without 13i); Commodities Spot D1 (two places, domestically, for the first time); Goods B4 (the
ground enters the same exponent the season enters — one formula, one site). Re-marked in COVERAGE
with a reading, not a mechanism: Cross-Border A1, B4, D1–D6, F1, F2 and Indices D1 are read as
**country**, and the rows say so.

## Why this is inserted before the rest of 13c

13c's remaining steps are: the location basis and a blocked-route scenario (D3, D3.a, D5), shipper
substitution including "source locally" (C2), and the producer/consumer price indices with freight
in them (Indices D4). Every one of those measures distance, and this world has **one producing
place** and four legs declared identical in `seeds/foundation.ts:222`:

```
transitPeriods: 4, unitsPerVesselPerPeriod: 25000, sailsIn: 4, sailsHardness: 8
```

on every leg. Freight A4 says capacity on one route is not capacity on another; today it is the same
route wearing four labels, and the three destinations are a central bank, a treasury and a bond line
until 13i. A basis test against that measures nothing, a "source locally" test has nowhere else to
source from, and both would be rewritten the day the map landed. The dependency runs one way — the
map needs nothing from the rest of 13c — so it goes first. Same shape as 10.3, 10.4, 11.5 and 11.6,
each inserted before the rest of an open item because the rest of that item was what reached them.

## The decision this item takes

### A region is a PLACE; a country is a MONEY

`RegionDecl` today is `{id, name, ccy}` and carries two jobs at once: it is where a thing is, and it
is which money that thing is in. The map needs many places per currency, so the two jobs separate:

- **`CountryDecl { id, name, ccy }`** — new kernel type. One currency, one central bank, one
  treasury, one sovereign line, one FX pair, one equity index. `ABROAD`'s `CountryDecl` in
  `seeds/foundation.ts` is already this shape; it is promoted to the kernel and gains its regions.
- **`RegionDecl { id, name, country }`** — `ccy` is **deleted**. The read that replaces it is
  `registry.currencyOf(region)`, which goes through the country. One writer for one fact (Law 4).

This is the cheap direction and the honest one. The economy is already written per region —
`goodId(subUnit, region)`, `goodMarketId`, `GoodTerms.region`, `WipTerms.region`,
`TechnologyDecl.terms.region`, `storageRateIn(reads, region)`, `conditionsIn(reads, region)`, the
`region` cell-key dimension, `PartyBase.region` — so making regions smaller makes all of it per-place
with **no renaming at all**. The 176 `.region` sites do not move. What moves is the ~25 sites that
spell `registry.region(x).ccy`, and the handful that assume one region per country: the central bank,
the treasury, the sovereign paper, the FX pair and the equity index, which re-point to the country.

Seed B3 ("the region determines its money") and Currency B1 ("the one its region uses") stay true as
written — a region determines its money uniquely, through its country. §39 Cross-Border and Indices
D1 do not: those say "region" and mean currency area. They are re-read as **country**, the COVERAGE
rows say so, and 13i gets the dependency note.

### What does NOT change

- No party kind, no instrument kind, no market kind, no phase ordering, no settlement rule.
- No new audit family. The map's invariants — every land tile in exactly one region, every region
  contiguous, no region on the frame — are checked at **assembly** and throw, because a world whose
  geometry is broken cannot run; they are not findings (see Error discipline, CLAUDE.md).
- **No cap on what can be built anywhere.** A region never refuses plant. See "Ground" below.

### What this does NOT make emerge: nobody chooses where to build

The map makes ground **heterogeneous** and distance **costly**, so grain is cheaper where the ground
is good and it moves to where it is not. What it does not build is a firm **deciding where to be**.
`TechnologyDecl.terms.region` is a literal today; this item gives it a REASON (the seed places a
line on ground that suits it, drawn) but not a DEATH — the death is a siting decision, and a siting
decision compares, per candidate region: what its ground yields, what the inputs cost delivered
there, what labour costs there, and what the output fetches delivered to its buyers. Two of those
four do not exist yet. Wages differ by place only once labour is per-place and people move (13d),
and a firm that can open in a region it is not in is the same decision a new firm makes about where
to be born (13g). **So siting is inserted at 13g**, after 13d has made the wage differ; until then a
region that is good at something stays good at it because the draw said so, and the record says that
plainly rather than calling it specialisation.

What DOES emerge from this item alone: two places print different prices for the same grade, the gap
is what somebody paid to move it, and a blocked leg widens it. That is Freight D3 and D5, and it is
the whole reason the item is here.

## Design

### Kernel: `registry/geography.ts` (new)

The name and the reads live in the kernel because four modules will need them — `freight` for legs,
`firms` for ground, `environment` for where a climate is drawn, the observer for the picture — and
four parsers would be four ways to misread one map (4.9b, the pattern `registry/environment.ts` and
`registry/physical.ts` already set).

```
export type TileIndex = Brand<number, 'TileIndex'>;
export type GroundKind = ...            // exactly the kinds a recipe names; today: 'arable'
export interface TerrainDecl {
  readonly id: string;                  // 'ocean' | 'plain' | 'hill' | 'mountain' | 'forest'
  readonly name: string;
  readonly kmPerDay: ParamId;           // TECHNOLOGY: how far a loaded carrier gets in a day
  readonly standsWind: ParamId;         // Freight B4: what a passage over it stands
  readonly windHardness: ParamId;       // how sharply it stops being passable
  readonly carriedBy: string;           // the capital kind that moves over it (VESSEL | VEHICLE)
  readonly ground: Readonly<Record<GroundKind, ParamId>>;  // as a multiple of ordinary ground
}
export interface GeographyDecl {
  readonly tileKm: number;              // RESOLUTION
  readonly cols: number; readonly rows: number;   // derived from the world's size in km
  readonly terrain: Int8Array;          // per tile, row-major: index into TERRAINS
  readonly owner: Int16Array;           // per tile: the region, or -1 for water
  readonly ground: Record<GroundKind, Float64Array>;  // per tile
}
```

Reads, all pure functions of the decl:

- `tilesOf(g, region)`, `areaKm2(g, region)`
- `touches(g, a, b)` — the two regions share a tile **edge** (4-neighbour). A shared corner is not
  a border.
- `portsOf(g, region)` — its land tiles that touch water.
- `leg(g, params, from, to): Leg | undefined` — the least-**day** path, 8-neighbour (a traveller may
  cut a corner even though a border may not), every tile of it sharing one `carriedBy`.
  `Leg = { km, days, carriedBy, standsWind, windHardness }`.
- `groundFor(g, params, region, kind, areaKm2InUse): number`
- `neighbourhood(g, region)` — the regions it touches, for the climate draw.

**The legs are computed once at assembly and held beside the grid.** That is memoisation of a pure
function of immutable data, not a second writer: the map never moves, the draw is its one writer, and
`leg()` is the only thing that can produce a leg (Law 4, Law 18 — traversal is free).

**Land and water are structural, not a flag.** An ocean tile belongs to no region; that IS the
distinction, and there is no `isWater` boolean to go stale.

### Kernel: `registry/registry.ts` (changed)

`CountryDecl` added; `RegionDecl.ccy` deleted, `RegionDecl.country` added; `RegistryDecl.geography`
added; `registry.country(id)` and `registry.currencyOf(region)` added. Assembly-time contract
violations: a region with no tiles, a region whose tiles are not contiguous, a land tile owned by no
region or by two, a region touching the frame, a country with no regions.

### The draw: `seeds/map.ts` (new)

A pure function with its own labelled stream — `prng(seed, 'geography')` — exactly as `drawClimate`
has `prng(seed, 'environment')`. It runs in `foundation.ts` **before** the registry is built, because
the regions it draws are registry declarations. It is not a module: the map is structure, not a
mechanism, and nothing in the period loop writes it.

1. **The frame.** `cols = round(worldWidthKm / tileKm)`, `rows` likewise. A height field from value
   noise; the lowest `oceanShare` of it becomes water; the outermost ring of tiles is forced to
   water, so nothing runs off the edge.
2. **The channels.** A drawn number of water walks from one edge to another, which is what splits the
   land into separate masses. Whether two countries end up sharing a coastline or an ocean is an
   **outcome of the draw**, not a setting.
3. **Regions by growth.** One seed tile per region, grown simultaneously by a randomised-frontier
   BFS so the shapes are irregular rather than hexagonal. Every land tile lands in exactly one.
4. **Countries by grouping.** Each country asks for a number of regions (its RESOLUTION); regions are
   assigned to countries in contiguous blocks. An island country is a legitimate outcome.
5. **Terrain.** Per tile from the height field plus its own noise, spatially correlated so mountains
   come in ranges and forests in stands.
6. **Ground.** Per tile per `GroundKind`, correlated in the same way and conditioned on terrain: a
   plain is good arable, a mountain is poor arable, and **every land tile's quality is positive** —
   there is no tile you cannot farm, only tiles it is not worth farming (Law 6).
7. **Names.** Display names drawn from a syllable table (TECHNOLOGY). Ids stay `us.3`; an internal id
   is never a display name (Law 9).

### Distance, time and cost

- **Kilometres.** A step to an edge neighbour is `tileKm`; to a corner neighbour, `tileKm * sqrt(2)`.
  A leg's `km` is the sum along the path.
- **Days.** A step costs `stepKm / kmPerDay(terrain of the tile entered)` days; the path minimises
  total days, and the leg's `days` is `upToTick(total, 1)` — a part-day on the road is a day, which
  is the day's own grain and the same idiom `core/tick.ts` already applies to a quantity's piece.
- **Arrival is a date, not a period count.** A cargo that sailed in period `p` is due at
  `addDays(calendar.startOf(p), leg.days)` and arrives when the period's own start is on or after it.
  `transitPeriods` and its parameter are **deleted**; the read that replaces them is `leg().days` on
  the calendar. A leg shorter than a period still arrives next period, because the arrive phase runs
  at cycle 0 before the session — which is the loop's grain and not a lie about the distance.
- **Passability.** `standsWind` and `windHardness` are the terrain's, and a leg takes the **least
  passable tile on its path** (`largest` in `core/num.ts` — a leg is as open as its worst point).
  The 13c arithmetic is unchanged: `exp(-(wind / standsWind) ^ hardness)`, positive at every wind,
  never one. The per-route parameters `sailsIn`/`sailsHardness` are deleted.
- **Cost, and only cost.** Two channels, both from the leg: the hull is **used up per unit-kilometre**
  (`wearPerUnitKm`, technology per capital kind), and the **crew is paid per day** through the
  carrier's existing labour scale. A third, which is capacity rather than cost: a hull makes
  `periodDays / (2 * days)` round trips per period, so what it can offer is `holdUnits` times that.
  Long legs are dearer three ways and every one of them is arithmetic. `unitsPerVesselPerPeriod` is
  deleted; `holdUnits` per capital kind replaces it.
- **Still no rate.** Nothing above prices anything. These are what a carrier POSTS AT; the session
  clears. 13c's test that greps the register for a freight rate stays, and gains the new params.

### Where a cargo is, and why it is a read

A leg's path is a fixed list of tiles and a cargo's lot carries the period it sailed, so **where it
is right now is a pure function of things that already exist**: walk the path to
`(today - sailed) / days`. The observer computes it; nothing stores it. So the picture can show every
cargo moving tile by tile with no new state, no second writer, and no display-only number — the path
and the dates are both read by mechanisms, and the position is arithmetic over them (Law 4, Law 19).

**Hulls and vehicles are counted, not positioned.** A carrier's plant is a stock in the register; a
leg's capacity is that stock times its round trips. Giving each hull a tile would be state that no
mechanism reads, which Appendix B forbids. The one thing that would earn it is a **chokepoint**: a
strait or a pass whose capacity is per-TILE, so two legs crossing the same tile compete for it and
Freight A4's "capacity on one route is not capacity on another" gains its counterpart — capacity on
a shared tile IS shared. That is a market per congested tile, and it is worth nothing until real
volume crosses one, so **it is inserted after 13i**, which is what puts that volume on the sea lanes.

### Ground reaches the yield

`Recipe.standsOn: GroundKind | null` — null is a real answer for a line made indoors (a mill, an
oven), exactly as an empty `exposedTo` is.

The quality a line stands on falls as more of the region is worked. `groundFor` walks the region's
tiles **best first**, and the answer is the quality of the **marginal** hectare at the area already
in use; past the last tile the same decline continues by the ratio the last two tiles set, which is
what working the same ground harder means. So:

- there is **no cap** — a region never refuses plant;
- the return from the next unit falls continuously and **never reaches zero**, so what stops
  expansion is the hurdle rate refusing the project, which is a decision a firm already makes;
- **rent emerges** instead of being assumed: good ground is scarce in quality, not in extent.

Area in use is `plantUnits * landPerUnit` (technology per capital kind), read over the register **at
the produce site**, where the season is already read, and never stored. A firm's *decision* uses what
its own last period yielded — its outlook — which is the pattern §46 already sets, so no new public
read is added at decide time.

The yield changes in exactly one place, `mechanisms/firms/produce.ts`, and it stays one formula:

```
pow(tech.yieldRate, 1 / (season * ground))
```

### Freight reads the map

`ROUTES` in `seeds/foundation.ts` is **deleted**. Legs are generated at assembly: a land leg for
every pair of regions that `touches`, a sea leg for every pair of regions with ports and a water
path. `RouteDecl` loses `transitPeriods`, `unitsPerVesselPerPeriod`, `sailsIn`, `sailsHardness` —
every one a read of the map now — and gains `carriedBy`, which is the capital kind the leg's terrain
declares. **Land versus sea is DATA, never a branch** (Law 15): a carrier owns vessels or vehicles,
a leg names the kind that moves over it, and no mechanism asks which.

`VEHICLE` joins `VESSEL` and `STORAGE` as a capital kind, with its own life, build lag, `standsWind`
and `holdUnits`. Carriers are drawn owning one kind or the other.

### The climate is drawn on the map

`drawClimate` takes a list of regions and draws each independently, which was right when regions were
countries and is wrong the moment two of them share a border. The memory and the width per fact are
drawn on the **grid** instead, so neighbouring regions have similar climates and distant ones do not.
The period's departure stays independent per region; **correlated weather shocks across neighbours
are named as 13h's**, where an insurer is the party that cares whether two claims are one event.

## Exit criteria

Two regions of the same country produce the same good at different prices, and the gap is what it
costs somebody to actually ship it; a blocked mountain pass widens that gap and it stays wide while
the pass is shut; a hull on a long leg offers less than the same hull on a short one; a farm on
better ground makes more from the same inputs, and the next farm in that region makes less than the
last; no region anywhere refuses to be built on; no freight rate exists in the register; the same
seed draws the same world, and halving the tile does not move a distance in kilometres.

## Guard

distinct); Law 2 (`tileKm` is a RESOLUTION and the invariance test is what says so); Law 3 (no
declared freight rate — distance is a cost, the session is the price); Law 4 (`ccy` has one writer,
the country); Law 6 (no cap on what a place can hold — the ground gets worse); Law 15 (terrain and
legs vary by DATA; no mechanism asks whether a leg is land or sea); Law 19 (every deleted parameter
names the read that replaces it); Appendix B, Observer (no display-only number: the reader test).
