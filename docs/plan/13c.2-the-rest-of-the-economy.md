# Item 13c.2 — The rest of the economy: services, distribution and retail

**Objective.** This world MAKES things and nothing else. Thirty-six physical lines, nine thousand
firms, and every one of them a farm, a mine, a mill or a works — which is between a fifth and a
quarter of what an economy is. The other three quarters is missing entirely: nobody treats a
patient, teaches a child, serves a meal, carries a passenger, cleans a building, repairs a machine,
writes a contract or answers a telephone; and between the factory gate and the household there is
nothing at all — no wholesaler, no lorry, no shop. A household in this world buys bread from a
bakery at the bakery's own cleared price, which is not how anybody has ever bought bread.

The gap is not a list of missing rows. It is three structures the model does not have:

1. **A thing that cannot be put in a box.** Every good here is storable, shippable and made to
   stock. A haircut, a diagnosis, a night's lodging and a lesson are none of those: they are made
   where they are bought, at the moment they are bought, and what is not sold is gone. That single
   TECHNOLOGY fact is what makes a service price LOCAL — it cannot be arbitraged by shipping,
   because there is nothing to ship — and local prices that cannot be arbitraged are most of why two
   regions with the same tradeable prices have different costs of living.
2. **The distance between the gate and the shelf.** A tonne of bread at a bakery and a loaf in a
   shop near where somebody lives are not the same thing, and the difference is real work done by
   real firms with real premises and real staff. Item 13c's own step 14 already names what is
   missing — "the price indices complete with freight and **the distribution margin**" — and there
   is no distribution margin because there is no distribution.
3. **A basket, and a preference that is a quantity.** `ConsumptionDecl.share` is a share of what a
   cohort SPENDS, so spending on a good never responds to that good's price. With one final good at
   `share: 1` that is inert; with twenty it is the strongest substitution assumption in the model and
   the exact mirror of what `phoenix/no-value-recipe` refuses on the production side. 13c's own
   dependency note says so: *"anything this item adds to the consumption basket carries that
   assumption."* This item adds eighteen things to the consumption basket, so this item pays for it.

**Where this is inserted, and why here** (Law 10). After 13c.1, which gave the world places, and
before the rest of 13c, for the same reason 13c.1 was: step 14 measures a consumer price index and a
distribution margin, and a world whose only consumer good is bread bought at the mill gate has
neither. Steps 10–13 of 13c (shipper substitution, the futures kind, its participants, the curve)
need nothing from this and are unchanged. It is NOT deferred to 13d: 13d moves people between places
and runs a household's life cycle, and both of those want a household that buys more than bread.

Labour A3, A3.a; Commodities Spot A3, C6, D1; Freight A3, C1.a, C2; Indices D4, D4.a; Law 2 (what a

**Clauses this item meets.** Goods A1, A2.a, A3, C1, C3, C5, G1.a, G1.b; Households A2.a, A2.b, C3,

### What is NOT built here, and where it goes

- **Passenger and freight movement WITHIN a region** is a service line (`transport`) that consumes
  fuel and vehicles and employs drivers. The thing it does NOT do is move goods between places on
  the map — that is freight's, it is built, and a second writer of the same fact is a Law 4 defect.
  A lorry with a tile-exact position is 13c.1's voyage machinery pointed at land, and it is
  positioned to 13d with labour mobility, which wants the same roads.
- **Banking, insurance and fund management** are services, and they are already parties with their
  own modules. They are not added as goods lines; a bank's output is its interest margin and its
  fees, and writing `banking` as a good with a recipe would be a second representation of one thing.
- **Government services** (a state that employs teachers and nurses directly) is 14's. What this
  item builds is the private tier; the polity buys from it or duplicates it, and that is a decision
  14 makes.
- **Substitution between goods**, as distinct from between quantities: a household facing dear
  butter does not buy margarine here. That is a decision nobody can observe, it is what Goods A2.a
  already refuses on the production side, and inventing an elasticity for it would be a fitted
  number wearing a preference's name.

### Steps

- [x] **A thing that cannot be put in a box.** `GoodDecl.portable` — declared TECHNOLOGY with its
      own `why`, on the good and never on the holder, because whether a thing can be moved is a fact
      about the thing. Freight reads it and carries what can be carried; a non-portable good never
      reaches a transit instrument, which is checked rather than assumed. Registry fault at
      assembly: a good that cannot be moved cannot be warehoused either, so `portable: false` with a
      `storagePerUnit` is a contradiction and throws where it is declared. Tests: the fact is
      declared for every line, a service never appears in `toShip`, and no transit instrument exists
      for one.
- [x] **A preference is a QUANTITY, and what a household spends is an outcome.**
      `ConsumptionDecl.share` is deleted. What a cohort declares per good is two quantities per
      member per period: what it will have before anything else (`needed`) and what it takes on top
      when it can (`wanted`). Both are PREFERENCE primitives in physical units — how much bread a
      person eats in a week — and neither is money. A cell fills its needs first out of its budget
      and spreads what is left across its wants in proportion, so the SHARE of income that goes on
      food falls as income rises with nothing stating that it does: an Engel curve as an outcome of
      two declared quantities. The schedule it posts is unchanged in shape (its own expected price,
      widened by its own surprises) and changed in size: the quantity is what it wants, not
      `budget / price`. Tests: spending shares differ between a rich cell and a poor one from the
      same table; a price rise in one good reduces the quantity bought of THAT good and of others
      only through the budget; the same table with one good reproduces what the world does today.
- [x] **Premises are the third kind of plant**, made from `building`, standing forty years. A shop,
      a surgery, a classroom, a restaurant and an office are one kind of structure to the arithmetic
      that wears it out, and declaring five would be five copies of one fact (Law 2). The land it
      stands on is what makes a town fill up (13c.1's `groundFor`).
- [x] **Sixteen service lines**, each a good that is not portable, is made to order (`leadTime: 0`),
      keeps nothing (`spoilage: 1` — what a surgery does not sell this week it cannot sell next
      week, which is why a service business has operating leverage and a warehouse does not), stands
      on no ground it does not build on, and is made from labour, premises and real inputs: care,
      teaching, hospitality, telecoms, software, professional work, design, media, transport,
      repair, facilities, personal care, entertainment, security, waste and logistics handling. Each
      has its own occupation with its own skill and sector (Labour A3.a: a nurse out of work is not
      a bricklayer's vacancy filled).
- [ ] **The shelf is a place.** For each of the nine goods a household actually buys in a shop, a
      retail line whose recipe is one unit of the wholesale good plus the labour, the premises, the
      power and the packaging that putting it on a shelf takes. It is a separate instrument with a
      separate market in each region, exactly as a tonne in transit is (13c step 8), because a tonne
      at the mill gate and a loaf in a shop are not one thing. The **distribution margin is then an
      outcome**: the gap between two prints, one of which is what the shop paid and the other what
      it got, less what the shop's own staff and premises cost it. Nothing declares a mark-up.
- [ ] **Retailers and service firms are FIRMS** (Law 15, the carrier precedent at 13c step 5): the
      same party kind, the same decision function, the same failure. What makes a firm a supermarket
      is the line it is in. The draw places them where the PEOPLE are rather than where the ground
      is good — a shop is built next to its customers and a mine is not — which is the first time
      siting differs by line without any mechanism branching on one.
- [ ] **Distributors: a firm whose business is the gap.** A merchant holds goods it did not make,
      buys them in the market of the place it is in, and offers them into freight for the place they
      are dear — so the location basis has a party whose whole purpose is to close it, and the
      freight session has a shipper that is not an accident of who happens to be holding stock. It
      owns warehouses and nothing else. Its margin is the basis less the freight less the wait, and
      it can be wrong: a cargo bought for a gap that has closed by the time it lands is a loss on a
      named book.
- [ ] Households buy the retail lines and the services and never the wholesale ones (Goods G1.a,
      G1.b: an intermediate price is one no household pays); firms buy the services their recipes
      name. The consumer and producer baskets stop being the same basket, which is what lets the two
      indices diverge for a reason.
- [ ] Observer: the economy by sector — what share of employment, of output and of the basket each
      of the twenty verticals is — read from the register and the journal, never stored.
- [ ] Tests written as the item goes and run at the end (PLAN, the three rules): a service is never
      shipped, never stored and never carried between periods; a shop's margin is two prints and its
      costs; the consumer index and the producer index move differently when freight moves; a
      service price in one region is free to differ from another's without limit and nothing closes
      it; the same seed gives the same world.
- [ ] Coverage re-marked; record entry; delete this file; worklist row → done; commit and push.

### Exit criteria

Every vertical a real economy has is either BUILT here, built already (banking, funds, insurance at
13h, the state at 14), or named above with the item that owns it. A household's basket is a basket.
Between the gate and the shelf there is a firm with staff and premises taking a margin nobody wrote
down. A service price is local because a service cannot be moved, and the model says why.

### Guard

The temptation is to write services as manufactures — a "business services" good with a bill of
materials, bought by the tonne. That is the category error this item exists to avoid, and the guard
against it is the `portable` fact plus the spoilage: a line that can be stored and shipped is a
manufacture whatever it is called, and one that can be neither is a service whatever it is called.
The second temptation is a declared retail mark-up, which would be a price from a formula (Law 3)
and a spread table (Appendix B). The margin is two prints or it is nothing.
