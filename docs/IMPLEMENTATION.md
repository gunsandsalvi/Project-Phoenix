# Project Phoenix — the implementation

> **This file is the ordered plan of what is left, rewritten on 2026-09-15 from a full adversarial
> review** of `docs/` against `packages/engine/src` (every doc read in full; every claim below was
> checked at the file and line it names; one rig world was assembled and stepped, once, to measure
> what the code does rather than what the record says it does). It replaces the file of the same
> name; `docs/RECORD.md` carries the review's entry.
>
> **One item at a time, in the order in Part 1 (Law 10).** An item is done when its exit holds, its
> steps are ticked, `docs/RECORD.md` has its entry and `docs/COVERAGE.md` is re-marked — one commit
> (Law 14). When an item closes, delete its section; the record says what it closed.
>
> **Every step below names a file, a change and a test.** A step that could be read two ways was
> rewritten until it could not. Where a step says *delete*, the deletion names the read that
> replaces it (Law 19). Where a step says *measure*, it says what number, from what read, at what
> scale.
>
> **The rule on tests, restated after what the review found.** Lint and typecheck are cheap and run
> whenever. The SUITE is a measurement and runs at the end of a module (Law 11). **But "does the
> world assemble and step" is not a measurement — it is the contract every module signed, and it is
> checked by `npm run check:opens` (item 0.9), which runs before lint in `npm run check` and may run
> at any time.** Eleven items closed on top of a world that threw at assembly; that rule is the
> one that stops it happening again.

### 0.3 What the world does when it opens

With the nine patches applied (thrown away after the run; they are item 0's work, done properly),
the rig stepped 22 periods. Scale: 3 banks, 18 firms, 12 household cells, 8 funds, 146 markets,
about 340 clearing sessions a period.

| period | ms | sessions cleared | noDemand | noSupply | noOverlap | events | parties |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 232 | 2 | 326 | 2 | 9 | 889 | 44 |
| 5 | 126 | 1 | 319 | 9 | 9 | 1,292 | — |
| 10 | 182 | 0 | 318 | 11 | 9 | 1,833 | — |
| 15 | 193 | 0 | 319 | 15 | 14 | 2,248 | — |
| 20 | 329 | 0 | 319 | 15 | 14 | 2,910 | 302 |
| 22 | 347 | 0 | 319 | 12 | 22 | 3,168 | — |

- **Nothing trades.** One or two sessions of ~340 clear in the first six periods; from period 7,
  none. `noDemand` is the outcome of 94% of every session every period. This is not a scale-model
  artefact: the record says the same of the full world (output falls to nothing by period 30;
  96 loans against 9,006 firms; every listed firm publishes a loss; no FX pair ever trades).
- **The population of parties grows 44 → 302 in 20 periods and never shrinks**: household cells
  split on every partial event (`weight` events 16–24 a period) and `cells.merge` has no caller.
  Cost per period grows with it (232 ms → 347 ms at this toy scale; the record's 25–60 s at full
  scale).
- **The event stream is dominated by `expectations.surprise`, `revaluation`, `households.lifecycle`,
  `housing.shortfall`, `fund.subscribed`** — bookkeeping about a world in which nobody buys, sells,
  borrows or builds. `housing.shortfall` fires for every cell every period: no household can get a
  mortgage (0.4, item 12a).
- **The audit total rises monotonically** (30 → 174 violations) and nothing reads it.

## Part 3 — Optimising the simulation to the core (Law 18)

### 3.1 What was measured

One CPU profile of the rig (3 banks, 18 firms, 12 cells → 302 parties, 146 markets) over 20
periods, 3.75 s total, plus the per-period census (0.3) and the record's full-scale figures
(25–60 s a period; 9,006 firms; 844 household cells by period 45; every cell holding every listed
line). Inclusive time by function (percent of run):

| share | where | what it is doing |
|---|---|---|
| 13.5% | `households/index.ts` `decide` + `willWork` | every cell, every period, every venue |
| 11.4% | `audit/audit.ts` `run` → `accounts.balanceSheet/sheetOf`, `flows.check` | every family re-walks every party's holdings and every instruction every period |
| 10.4% | `ledger/settlement.ts` `settle/apply/validate/precheck` | the wire; proportional to legs (fine) |
| 10.2% + 8.9% | `world.ts` `gather` + `runOne` | for EVERY market, for EVERY participant declaration, for EVERY party of that kind: build a view, ask for orders |
| 5.8% | `households/lifecycle.ts` `settleEstates` | probate every period |
| 4.8% | `participantView` / `buildParticipantView` | ~30 closures per party per cycle |
| 4.1% | `world.ts` `asked` | the per-cycle index of who was asked |
| 3.8% | `outlookOf` | outlook lookups |
| 3.1% | `worldReads` getter | rebuilt per read |
| 2.5% + 1.4% | `core/num.ts` `get`, `core/measure.ts` `get` | runtime wrappers around numbers |
| 1.4% | `reach.ts` `produced` | tally on every post |
| 9% | GC | the closures, the wrappers, the frozen event objects |

At full scale the same shape is quadratic in two places the profile cannot show at 3 banks: the
party count (cells split every period, nothing merges) and cells × listed lines (every cell holds
and revalues every line). The record's 12c.1-1 (16 → 844 cells in 45 periods) and 11.6 (every cell
holds every line) are the two.

### 3.2 The resolution ladder — the gate for everything in this part

Law 2: a count of banks, firms, members, countries is a RESOLUTION, tested by invariance. Law 18:
gate on behaviour, not bits. So the first step is a test, and every later step is gated on it:

- `test/ladder.test.ts` (0e.1): run 52 periods at (3, 12), (6, 60), (12, 200) banks/firms; record
  per scale: ms/period at periods 1, 13, 26, 52; parties; events; sessions cleared; the audit
  total; money stock per member; wage per hour (the level, Law 8). Assert the ratios that must be
  scale-invariant are (money per member; wage; sessions cleared per market) within DUST derived from
  the run's magnitudes, and print the rest. Time is reported, never asserted.
- Every P-step below reports the ladder before and after in its record entry. A step that changes
  a ratio is not a performance step and is reverted.

### 3.3 The steps, in order of yield per line changed

- **P1 — Gather by declaration, not by product.** `world.ts:gather`/`runOne` loop every
  `participantDecl` × every market × every party of the kind. Index declarations at assembly by
  `(partyKind, marketKind | venueKind)`; a market asks only the kinds declared for its kind; a
  party is asked once per period per market it can post in. Expected: gather + runOne from 19% to
  under 5%; at full scale (hundreds of markets × thousands of parties) this is the dominant term.
- **P2 — The audit walks what changed.** `accounts`, `flows`, `ownership`, `money` recompute every
  balance sheet every period. The register and the ledger are the one writer of each fact; keep a
  per-party dirty set written by settlement and revaluation, and let each family read the sheets of
  dirty parties plus the totals it keeps as running sums with the same dust tolerance (Law 7,
  unchanged). The invariant is unchanged; the traversal is. Expected: 11% → ~2%.
- **P3 — Period-scoped journal reads.** `view.failedPayments(0)`, `lastPublic` filtering
  `ofKind`, `measuredMove` re-walking history, `reach()` re-walking every price history each audit:
  every one becomes a read of an index the writer maintains (`byKindPeriod` exists; add
  `bySubjectKindLatest` and a per-instrument move accumulator written at the print). Item 12a.2
  deletes the worst of them by mechanism.
- **P4 — One view per party per PERIOD.** `buildParticipantView` allocates ~30 closures per party
  per cycle; `self` is already a live getter. Make the whole view live (read `world.currentCycle`
  through the getter) and cache per party for the period; `mechanismContext` likewise. Expected:
  4.8% + GC.
- **P5 — The register's per-party index is a Map of Maps, never filtered arrays.** `holdingsOf`,
  `issuedBy`, `quantity` must be O(1) lookups; `snapshot` (0 callers) deleted.
- **P6 — The price store answers `latest` in O(1)** per (market, instrument) with the history in a
  typed array per series; `index()` cached by version already — keep.
- **P7 — The reach tally counts at assembly and increments a number**, not a Map lookup per post.
- **P8 — `nextSplitId` keeps a counter per cell** instead of probing `parties.has` linearly.
- **P9 — Numbers without runtime wrappers.** `Measure<D>` and `num.ts` `get` are 4% + GC. Keep the
  TYPES (branded numbers checked by the compiler) and drop the objects: a `Measure` is a `number &
  { __d: D }`; `add/minus/scale` are plain arithmetic with the dust rule unchanged. Mechanical, large,
  and the ladder is the only gate.
- **P10 — The solver's candidate scan** (`clearing/solver.ts:clear`) recomputes filtered sums per
  candidate price (O(prices × orders)). Sort once, sweep once with running sums (O(n log n)); pro
  rata and `splitOnTick` unchanged; outcomes byte-identical (assert on a fixed book).
- **P11 — Cells × lines is a mechanism, not a layout** (22a.3): households open holding two
  lines, not every line; a cell that never bought a share does not revalue it. Named here because
  it is the largest cost at full scale and the ladder will show it; done at 22a.
- **P12 — Merge (12.5)** is the other half of the quadratic party count and is a mechanism; named
  here, done at 12.5.

### 3.4 The gate

At (12, 200) banks/firms, one country: **a 52-period year in under 60 s on the CI runner** with
every ladder ratio invariant across the three scales; at (3, 12): under 3 s. The app (24) ships the
smallest scale the ladder shows invariant. What is NOT done: no mechanism is simplified, no
tolerance widened, no phase skipped, no event dropped from the journal (Law 8: the history is the
number), no sampling of parties.

## Appendix — the lessons this file exists to keep

1. **A `done` row and a `MET` mark are claims.** Eleven items closed on a world that threw at
   assembly. `check:opens` is the falsifier and it runs before lint.
2. **"Do not measure mid-build" is not "never measure".** Assembly is a contract; the suite is a
   measurement; both have a time, and the second's time came and went eleven times.
3. **A missing sector is an item; a defect is evidence.** Filing an absence as a finding under a
   done item lost six sectors once.
4. **A finding leaves this file only by its item closing**, as a ticked step that names it.
5. **A performance step that changes a ratio on the ladder is not a performance step.**
6. **Insert at the dependency position and say where** (`0a`, `12a`, `18a`, `22a`), never append.
7. **A test that names a party is asserting a world that no longer exists.** Ask the draw.
8. **A stated opening price is an imported equilibrium** however small the number.
